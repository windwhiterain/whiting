# Chongqing–Sichuan stilt houses (吊脚楼) and the river market town (场镇): structural and geometric rules for a procedural model

**Question.** What structural and geometric rules define the Chongqing–Sichuan stilt house
(吊脚楼, *diàojiǎo lóu*) and the riverside market town (场镇, *chǎngzhèn*) it stands in, precisely
enough to drive a procedural model — and which of the current flat-plate volume vocabulary's
assumptions break on them?

**Date.** 2026-10-18. Every URL in §6 carries a reliability tag. A structural conclusion is only
drawn where the tag is `[heritage survey]` or `[academic]`; `[travel/blog]` and `[unverified]` may
be quoted for vocabulary and for the names of examples, never for a number that carries weight.
A dimension no qualifying source states is written **not established**, together with the
measurement that would settle it, and is never guessed.

**Scope note.** This is one slice of a larger survey. The south-west minority dwelling (Yunnan,
Guizhou), the general Chinese vernacular timber frame and the official/official-style buildings
built to 营造法式 are covered elsewhere or only appear here as a **measuring standard**.

## Verdict

The Chongqing/Sichuan house is not a mass on a base; it is a **column grid standing on independent
stone pads at individually chosen heights**, so the floor of a 穿斗 (*chuàndòu*, "pierced-through")
timber frame floats over a slope and the ground is fitted to the building rather than the reverse.
A 吊脚楼 is the **半干栏** (*bàn gānlán*, "half-stilt") case: the main range 正屋 (*zhèngwū*) stands
on the ground while the wing 厢房 (*xiāngfáng*) hangs on posts over it, which is what distinguishes
it from a full 干栏 (*gānlán*) where *every* column is off the ground [S1]. What a generator must
have, in order of how badly the current vocabulary misses it: **per-column free length with no
common storey datum**; **an inclined-brace member as a first-class object** (斜撑 *xiéchēng* / 撑弓
*chēnggōng*), because a timber frame is braced by struts and never by a rigid shear panel; **a roof
as a pitched double surface with a ridge direction that need not match the plan grid**, carried on
檩 (*lǐn*, purlins) that sit directly on the columns with no tie beam; **a cantilevered gallery
plate with a distinct bracket zone** (挑廊 *tiāoláng* / 走马廊 *zǒumǎláng*); **steps and quay
flights as objects with pitch and count** (踏步 *tàbù*); and **a party wall that rises through the
roof plane** (封火墙 *fēnghuǒqiáng*). Four assumptions of the current vocabulary break outright: a
**wall ring with a level base** (here the "base" is point supports at unequal heights, and the ring
is a row of columns, not a thick wall); **flat plates for every roof** (悬山 roofs are pitched at
roughly 四分水–五分水, about 22°–27° [S1], with eaves over 1 m and front galleries around 3 m deep);
**a courtyard as a ring cut into the mass** (in this type the 天井 is unroofed *ground* left between
separate ranges that merely touch); and **openings attached to named faces of a ring** (the faces
that matter here are the eave line, the column bays and the ground between the stilts). The town is
governed by a second rule the type and its town share: **water arrives along the street, not across
it** — in the 2020 磁器口 flood the river ran 150 m up the main street and overtopped 670-odd houses
[S9] — so a town generator has to band its ranges by distance from the water and treat the first
row's ground floor as sacrificial. Finally, a warning about the evidence: **exactly one qualifying
source in this entire slice carries dimensions for the frame** [S1], and it is a comparative survey
that does not name the buildings it measured, so every number in §3 is a *range*, and the town's own
dimensions are almost entirely absent (§3.5).

## The parts and their correspondence

Terms are given as 汉字 + pinyin + English gloss at first use. All observations below are drawn
from [S1] unless marked; [S1] is a dissertation that states its own method ("after comparing a large
amount of surveying data"), and it is the **only** qualifying source found this session that
carries dimensions for the Chongqing frame. Where [S1] is silent, the gap is recorded rather than
filled.

### 1.1 Ground and site

- **坡地** (*pōdì*, sloping ground) — the default. [S1] reports the settlement rule that the
  vernacular building "develops from top to bottom" (自上而下), building up the slope and hanging
  down it, rather than levelling a platform.
- **点基础** (*diǎn jīzhǔ*, point foundation) — [S1]: "the contact between stilted buildings and the
  slope is reduced to a few points". This is the single most important site fact for a generator:
  the building does not meet the ground continuously.
- **石墩 / 柱础** (*shídūn / zhǔchǔ*, stone pier / column base) — a stone pad under each wooden
  column. [S1] records a **勒脚** (*lèjiǎo*, plinth course) under the columns, carved on wealthy
  houses and **absent on poor rural houses**; that is a wealth parameter, not a constant.
- **条形基础** (*tiáoxíng jīzhǔ*, strip foundation) and **满铺基础** (*mǎnpū jīzhǔ*, spread
  foundation) — [S1] names both: the strip foundation is dug as trenches following the column grid
  and filled with 灰土 (*huītǔ*, grey soil), 三合土 (*sānhétǔ*, three-way lime/soil/sand mix) and
  concrete; the spread foundation paves the whole base with grey soil below and stone above, is
  "moisture-proof" but "the cost is also very high". Which one — not established as a rule.
- **洪水位 / 临水吊脚** (flood level; waterside stilt) — the vertical escape the type is named for.
  The one hard dataset found is for **磁器口 in the 2020 flood** [S9]: peak water level **192.65 m**,
  which was **11.65 m above the warning level (警戒水位)** and **8.65 m above the guaranteed/design
  level (保证水位)** — so, by subtraction, the warning level is 181.00 m and the guaranteed level
  184.00 m (computed, not printed). The river ran **150 m up 磁器口正街** into the town and
  **670-odd houses were overtopped**. This is the flood-storey logic stated as an event rather than
  as a rule: the ground floor of the first row is the storey that gets wet, and the value of hanging
  the *upper* floor is that it stays dry. A house floor height above the guaranteed level is
  **still not established** — settle it by levelling a bank section against the authority's
  published 警戒/保证水位.
- **堡坎** (*bǎokǎn*, stone revetment between two terrace levels) — [S10], the Chongqing
  government's 2015 approval of the 洪安 protection plan, names it explicitly as one of the elements
  that make up the town's historic character: "保护构成历史风貌的各个要素（包括建筑、语录塔
  (*yǔlù tǎ*, an inscribed-slogan tower)、**码头、堡坎**、树木等）". So the revetment is a *listed constituent of the heritage fabric*, not a detail — but its
  height, batter and build-up are **not established** in any source found; settle by levelled
  sections along the 洪安老街 bank and the 磁器口 wharf.

### 1.2 The frame: what is carried by which member

- **柱** (*zhù*, column) is the only thing that reaches the ground. [S1] distinguishes
  **落地柱** (*luòdìzhù*, column standing on the ground) from **吊脚柱** (*diàojiǎozhù*, hanging
  column) and states the rule: "Under normal circumstances, the floor column is relatively thick,
  and the diameter of the non-floor column should not be larger than the diameter of the floor
  column" — a non-floor column is never thicker than a floor column.
- **穿枋** (*chuānfáng*, through-tie) — the horizontal member that passes **through** the column and
  ties the whole grid into one frame. In 穿斗 construction the columns are connected by 穿枋 to form
  a single frame, and the 斗枋 (*dòufáng*, the purlin-bearing longitudinal tie, "dou") runs
  perpendicular to it; 檩 are then placed **on the columns**. [S1] is explicit: the roof is carried
  on purlins sitting on columns, with **no tie beam (梁) and no architrave** in the bay — that is
  what makes the frame itself the wall line and the infill replaceable.
- **斜撑** (*xiéchēng*, inclined strut) and **撑弓** (*chēnggōng*, bow brace) — the bracing. **Reach,
  angle and spacing: not established.** [S1] names no dimension for either. These are the members
  whose absence from the current vocabulary is the single most damaging gap, because a diagonal
  strut cannot be expressed as an offset on a plate.
- **檩** (*lǐn*, purlin) — 脊檩 (*jǐlǐn*, ridge purlin), 金檩 (*jīnlǐn*, intermediate purlin),
  檐檩 (*yánlǐn*, eave purlin), 挑檐檩 (*tiāoyánlǐn*, the outrigger purlin that carries the eave).
  [S1] gives their diameters as **multiples of the eave-column diameter** — the only dimension law
  in the whole survey that is stated as a rule rather than a measurement.
- **檐柱 / 落地柱** naming caution: [S1] uses "ride column" for the non-floor column, which is the
  English rendering of 吊脚柱. Where this note says *stilt column* it means 吊脚柱.

### 1.3 Enclosure (not structure)

- **板壁 / 木板壁** (*bǎnbì*, board wall) — boards about **12 cm wide and 3 cm thick**, built into
  the frame between columns and 穿枋; often painted with 桐油 (*tóngyóu*, tung oil) earth paint,
  often left bare [S1].
- **竹编夹泥墙** (*zhúbiān jiānínqiáng*, bamboo lattice with clay render) — named in the brief and
  in regional writing; [S1] describes the rammed-earth wall as containing pre-placed bamboo bars
  (*"Bamboo bars are arranged in the wall in advance, and then white plaster is applied on the wall
  after it is solidified"*) — i.e. the 竹编 is the reinforcement **inside** the 版筑 earth wall, not
  a separate wall type in this account.
- **版筑土墙** (*bǎnzhú tǔqiáng*, rammed earth wall) — thickness **370–400 mm**, with a
  **青石** (*qīngshí*, bluestone) base course under the earth so the wall is not eroded by moisture
  [S1]. Brick walls in Chongqing are used mainly in 山墙 (*shānqiáng*, gables), screen walls and the
  defensive parts of a manor, and spread widely only from the late Qing onwards [S1].
- **封火墙** (*fēnghuǒqiáng*, fire-partition wall) — a party wall that rises above the roof.
  [S3] (a county-government heritage page, no dimensions) states the practice in 洪安老街: two
  houses are separated by a 封火墙 "higher than the roof by several metres". **Height above the
  ridge: not established**; "several metres" is a travel-register phrase and carries no number.
- **天井** (*tiānjǐng*, skywell) — the Chongqing vernacular 天井 is described by the 地方志 as
  "十分窄小 … 仅供采光通风所用" (very small, for light and ventilation only) [S2]. **Size and
  count per lot: not established** in any qualifying source found this session.

### 1.4 The roof

- **悬山** (*xuánshān*, overhanging-gable roof) — dominant; 硬山 (*yìngshān*, flush gable) and
  庑殿 (*wǔdiàn*, hipped) are rare, because rain in Chongqing erodes a flush gable, which is exactly
  why the eaves overhang the gable side [S1].
- **小青瓦** (*xiǎoqīngwǎ*, small grey clay tile) — laid **directly on the rafters with no sheathing**
  (冷摊瓦 *lěngtānwǎ*, "cold-laid tiles" — [S1]'s English renders it "cold distribution tile"), which
  is why the underside shows tile backs and the
  roof ventilates. **Tile dimensions: not established** — [S1] says so explicitly: "The size of
  tile varies greatly from region to region and it is difficult to give an accurate size. The reason
  may be that there has never been a standard for making tiles."
- **出檐** (*chūyán*, eave overhang) — three separate depths, all from [S1]: the front eave forms a
  corridor about **3 m** wide; the small eave from the 封檐板 (*fēngyánbǎn*, eave board) to the wall
  face is **more than 1 m**; and on houses with board walls the gable-side eave exceeds **1.5 m** so
  that it covers the gable against rain.
- **腰檐 / 披檐** (*yāoyán / pīyán*, lower/intermediate eave, skirt eave) — the multi-storey front
  gets a separate eave per storey. Named in the brief and consistent with [S1]'s "add eaves" for
  south-eastern minority roofs; **no dimension found in a qualifying source.**

### 1.5 The river market town (场镇)

- **街 / 巷** (*jiē / xiàng*, main street / lane) — [S10] makes "保护洪安镇传统街巷整体空间格局和
  **尺度**" (protect the overall spatial pattern **and scale** of the town's streets and lanes) a
  statutory obligation, and [S9] lists 街道空间 (*jiēdào kōngjiān*, street space) and **头排建筑**
  (*tóupái jiànzhù*, the first row of buildings fronting the street) as the two spatial
  value-elements of the street category. So the street is treated as a space **plus a frontage
  row**, which is exactly the shop-house terrace that a generator must be able to emit as a
  repeated unit. **Widths: not established.**
- **码头** (*mǎtóu*, wharf) — a listed heritage element in 洪安 [S10], and the north boundary of its
  core protection zone is drawn along 洪安码头. It is a first-class town object, not paving.
  **Geometry: not established.**
- **踏步 / 街梯** (*tàbù*, bank and street steps) — [S3] records 洪安老街 as a **stair** ("青石板
  铺就的阶梯呈缓坡") running the full length of the street down to the ferry. **Riser, tread and
  pitch: not established.**
- **过街楼** (*guòjiēlóu*, building bridging the street), **院坝** (*yuànbà*, yard), **骑楼**
  (*qílóu*, arcade over the pavement) — named in the brief; **no qualifying source found for any of
  them this session**, and none is asserted in §3.
- **铺面 / 店宅** (*pùmiàn / diànzhái*, shop front / shop-house) — the one qualitative statement
  retrieved is a snippet from an **unidentified Chinese journal** hosted on hsfwest.com, 2024
  issue 4 [S13]: "街铺面通常为一至两开间，少数为三间及以上的大型店铺" — street shopfronts are
  usually **one or two bays**, a few are three or more for large shops. This is consistent with the
  3–4.5 m bay of §3.1 and therefore implies a frontage module of roughly **3–9 m**, but the source
  is tagged **[unverified]** and the journal could not be identified, so it is recorded as a
  **plausible cross-check, not as a measurement**.

### 3.6 One descriptive figure, marked as such

[S3] (秀山县人民政府, 洪安老街, 2025-11-26, **descriptive register, no dimensions, unverified for
structure**) gives: the street covers **8万多 m²**, runs **1000多米** long, is paved in bluestone
steps forming a gentle slope, and runs down to a ferry landing (拉拉渡口); the houses are 土家/苗族
吊脚楼 in 木质穿斗 with 小青瓦 roofs. These are inventory figures for one street, not rules; a
generator must not treat "1000 m" as a town length.

### 3.7 Flood datum for 磁器口, 2020 — the only measured flood in this slice

All five numbers are from [S9], an academic paper by 李和平 (Li Heping, Chongqing University) and
colleagues in 《城市规划学刊》; the two levels in the last row are **computed by subtraction here**,
not printed by the source.

| quantity | value | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| peak flood level, 2020, at 磁器口 (嘉陵江) | **192.65 m** | printed by [S9] |
| above warning level 警戒水位 | **11.65 m** | printed |
| above guaranteed level 保证水位 | **8.65 m** | printed |
| warning level 警戒水位 | **≈181.00 m** | computed: 192.65 − 11.65 |
| guaranteed level 保证水位 | **≈184.00 m** | computed: 192.65 − 8.65 |
| inundation up 磁器口正街 from the river | **150 m** | printed; this is the *intrusion length along the street*, not a depth |
| houses overtopped | **670 余间** (670-odd) | printed |
| flood scenarios modelled | 10-, 50- and 100-year return periods, 2-D unsteady flow, **HEC-RAS** | printed; risk classes: 10-year = 洪涝高风险, 50-year = 较高, 100-year = 中风险 |

**How to read this for a model.** The flood arrives *along the street axis*, not across it: 150 m of
main street was under water. That is the geometric statement that a town generator must reproduce —
the street is a flood channel, so the ground floor of the first row is sacrificial, and the mass of
the town is carried by the row behind and above. Depth at the water's edge is **not established**.

### 3.8 Town-scale planning control at 洪安 — the only statutory figures found

From [S10], 《重庆市人民政府关于秀山土家族苗族自治县洪安历史文化名镇保护规划的批复》
(渝府〔2015〕33号, 2015-06-23). These are **planning controls on new work**, not measurements of the
historic houses — but they bound what a generator should produce, and they state which objects the
authority considers part of the town.

| zone | area | control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| planning area 规划范围 | **≈627.06 ha** | east to 清水江, south to the 渝黔 border and G319, west to 两岔河水库, north to 贵塘村 |
| 重点保护区 core zone | **≈4.48 ha** | east = boundary control line of the heritage units and historic buildings on the east side of 洪安老街; south = G319; west = 洪安河; north = 三不管岛 and **洪安码头** |
| 建设控制区 construction control zone | **≈36.58 ha** | new buildings in local ethnic form; **main new buildings ≤ 12 m** |
| 环境协调区 coordination zone | **≈586 ha** | small massing, no interruption of the skyline or the view corridors |

Height rules stated in the core zone: **no new or extended buildings except necessary
infrastructure and public service, which must be below 9 m**, and **no new residential buildings at
all** inside the core zone; recent buildings inconsistent with the traditional character must be
altered, moved or demolished [S10]. **What the control implies for a model: the whole traditional
fabric of a protected stilt-house town fits under roughly 9 m**, which at a ~3 m storey is two
storeys plus roof — a useful consistency check on the storey count a generator invents.

The core-zone boundary is itself a modelling lesson: it is drawn on the **east side of the street
only**, so the protected object is the *first row* (头排) and the river front, not the whole town.

## How the type varies

### 4.1 By structural idea (the axis that matters for a generator)

| variant | what is off the ground | source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **干栏** (*gānlán*, full stilt) | the whole building; every column is a stilt column | [S1]'s taxonomy |
| **半干栏 / 吊脚楼** (*bàn gānlán / diàojiǎolóu*) | part only: the main range on the ground, the wing hanging — [S1] "a form of stilted building, namely 'semi-stilted building'" | [S1] |
| **全柱落地** (*quánzhù luòdì*, all columns on the ground) | nothing; the depth is simply spacing × bays | [S1] |

### 4.2 By region

- South-east Chongqing (黔江, 酉阳, 秀山/洪安, 武隆): the stilted form is "the architectural image
  representative of southeast Chongqing", and [S1] says the 土家, 苗 and 仡佬 stilt traditions
  differ from each other in "terrain utilization, shape, overhead, details and roofs" — five
  separate axes of variation, none of them dimensioned.
- Wushan and other high-altitude areas: rammed earth walls rather than timber-and-board walls,
  because "the temperature difference between morning and evening is large … and the tree resources
  are limited" [S1]. This is a **climate-driven substitution of the infill, not of the frame.**
- Daba Mountains: stone for walls and tiles [S1].
- Economically developed towns: brick-wood, with brick columns and brick walls carrying the timber
  roof frame [S1] — the frame survives, the body changes.

### 4.3 By century

- The **courtyard (四合) plan is the wealth marker**: [S1] states it is "a type only the upper class
  and rich people can afford and use to show their social status" and that most such houses were
  destroyed in wars and revolutions. So: most extant fabric is 一/L/三, and 四 is rare and should be
  generated rarely.
- Late Qing → modern: brick spreads from gables into whole walls, "because of the increasing demand
  for wood" [S1]. A generator parameter `wall_material` should therefore be free to vary over time.
- Post-1990: new 仿古 ("old-style") work in the towns is explicitly criticised by [S1] — "many new
  antique buildings tend to ignore environmental factors in pursuit of a larger building area. In
  some buildings of new ancient towns, there is even no space for green plants". Treat any
  post-1990 building in a 场镇 as, by default, **not** a model of the type.

### 4.4 Parameters a generator should expose vs fix

**Expose:** number of hanging storeys; which ranges are on the ground and which hang; per-column
pad height; front gallery depth; eave depth on the three axes (front, side, rear); wall material
(earth / board / brick / bamboo-and-clay); number of skywells; plinth present/absent; plan family
(一/三/四); roof ridge direction relative to the plan; slope (三分水…五分水); party-wall/fire-wall
presence.

**Fix (they are structural constants, not choices):** column grillage running the full depth with
purlin on column and no tie beam; suspended column never thicker than a floor column; purlin
diameters as multiples of the eave-column diameter; roof slope in the 三分水–五分水 band; 悬山 as the
overwhelming default.

### 5.2 Constructs the current flat-plate vocabulary lacks, named as constructs

1. **Column grid with independent base heights.** A "wall ring" implies a constant footprint edge;
   here the footprint edge *is* the set of column axes, and each column carries its own length.
   Needed: a *column grid* object whose members have individual heights, not a ring whose members
   share a plan offset.
2. **Point support on a terrain.** The building meets the ground at points (柱础), so the ground is
   an independent solid that the building does not own. Needed: terrain as its own surface with a
   cut/fill relation to each pad.
3. **Inclined strut as a member.** 斜撑 / 撑弓 is a *straight member between two nodes*, not an
   offset applied to a face. Needed: a two-node strut primitive (same family as the loft, not the
   polygon-offset family).
4. **Pitched double surface with a free ridge axis.** Every roof here is two planes meeting at a
   ridge whose direction is chosen per building and often does not follow the plan grid. Needed: a
   *ridge object* (a line with a plan direction and two eave lines), which then generates the two
   planes.
5. **Purlin carried directly on column, no beam.** A plate "slab" between columns does not exist
   here; the thing between columns is open air plus infill. Needed: infill as an optional, separately
   toggleable skin rather than as the wall.
6. **Cantilevered gallery with a bracket zone.** 挑廊 is a plate whose inner edge is on the column
   line and whose outer edge is on the eave line, supported by 挑枋 / 撑弓 that start *below* the
   plate. Needed: a cantilever construct with a distinct support zone, not a wider floor plate.
7. **Party wall passing through the roof plane.** 封火墙 rises above the ridge; a wall ring closed
   at the eave cannot express it. Needed: a wall that can be *taller than the roof solid* and
   continue past it.
8. **Steps as counted objects.** 踏步 and stair flights are separate solids with rise, tread,
   width, and a count; the street itself is a stair. Needed: a stair/flight primitive with a count,
   not a ramp or an extruded profile.
9. **Unroofed ground between ranges as a space, not a hole.** The 天井 of this type is *land*; the
   current "courtyard cut into a mass" is a void inside a solid. Needed: a lot = a set of separated
   masses + the ground between them.
10. **Independent per-storey eaves (腰檐/披檐).** A multi-storey front stacks eaves, each its own
    overhang. Needed: eaves as per-storey geometry attached to a level, not one per building.
11. **A flood gradient across the town, not a site plane.** [S9] shows the water entering *along*
    the main street for 150 m; the town is therefore ordered by distance-from-river, and the first
    row is a different kind of object from the rows behind it. Needed: a town generator that places
    ranges in bands of distance from the water and assigns the ground floor of band 0 differently.
12. **A protected-zone as a first-class region.** [S10] draws the core zone along one side of one
    street and along the wharf — the protected thing is a *frontage row plus a river edge*, not a
    bounding polygon around a town. Needed: a region whose boundary follows the street frontage and
    the water, if the model is ever to carry the statutory reading.

  storage under the floor) but **no source found gives a height, a count, or a spacing for the
  stilts**; [S1] gives only the column diameters. This is the most-repeated number-free claim in
  the literature and it is left empty rather than filled. Settle by: one measured elevation of a
  hanging range with the ground line drawn on it.
- **The "three fraction / four fraction / five fraction of water" convention** is reported here from
  [S1]'s English text only. The Chinese term is 分水 / 几分水; a Chinese-language source should be
  found before the convention is implemented, because the fraction is defined against the
  檐檩→脊檩 horizontal run in [S1] and other trades define it against the roof plane.
- **Method limitation, recorded because it affects confidence in §3**: the numbers in §3.1–3.3 were
  read out of a PDF by a purpose-written extractor, not from the typeset page. Every number quoted
  was checked against its sentence in context and the arithmetic in the ratio tables checks
  (0.4 → 21.8°, 0.5 → 26.6°, 1:1.5 rafter ratio), but the **figures themselves are images in that
  PDF and were not seen**. Any number that drives a design decision should be confirmed against the
  printed page of [S1] or against an independent survey before it is trusted.
