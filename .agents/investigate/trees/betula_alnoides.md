# 西南桦 — Betula alnoides

*Measured 2026-10-18. One question: what does this tree look like, and how does it grow?*

> **Two corrections to the brief, from the source.** (1) 中国植物志 describes the bark as **红褐色 — reddish-brown**, not white. What the photographs show is a pale papery outer bark peeling away to expose a **cinnamon/chestnut underlayer**; the white-bark birches of the Himalaya (*Betula utilis*) are a different species and must not be used as a model for this one. (2) FRPS gives no trunk diameter and does not call this tree a pioneer; its Chinese range is only **southern Yunnan and Hainan (尖峰岭)** on hillside mixed forest at 700–2100 m, not the high-montane zone the word "montane pioneer" suggests. The bark in reference image `02` is the best available evidence and supports the reddish-brown reading.

## Site

- **Altitude:** **700–2100 m.**
- **Habitat:** 山坡杂林 — mixed forest on hillslopes.
- **Provinces:** southern Yunnan; Hainan (Jianfengling), Guangdong. Also Vietnam and Nepal.
- **Vegetation belt:** warm-temperate to subtropical montane mixed forest, not the cold high-altitude birch belt. Note the range includes **Hainan**, which confirms a relatively warm, low-latitude birch.
- **Grows with:** FRPS does not name associates. *Betula* generally colonises disturbed, open ground, but **that is a genus-level expectation, not something this entry states** — do not model it into the note as a measured fact.

## Habit

- **Mature height: to 16 m** (乔木，高达16米). That is short for a tree of this stature — it is a slender, small-crowned forest tree, not a dominant canopy giant.
- **Trunk diameter:** **not stated.** See the modelling table.
- **Silhouette, one sentence:** a slim, small tree whose thin pendulous branchlets hang down from a light, open crown, so the whole outline is airy and weeping rather than dense.
- **Crown change with age:** *Estimate:* a young tree is a narrow spire; with age the leader is lost, the crown widens only modestly (the 16 m height ceiling implies a crown far narrower than it is tall), and the branchlets lengthen and droop.

## Branching

- **Branches:** 暗紫褐色，**有条棱 (ridged)**, glabrous (枝条暗紫褐色，有条棱，无毛). The ridged character of the twigs is a real, usable surface detail.
- **Young branchlets (小枝):** densely covered with **white long soft hairs and resin glands** (密被白色长柔毛和树脂腺体) — the shoots are conspicuously whitish-felted when young.
- **Phyllotaxy:** not stated by FRPS. **Estimate:** alternate and spirally arranged, as is typical of *Betula* — visible in the branch photographs.
- **First-order branch angle:** **drooping.** FRPS does not state it; *Estimate* from the reference photographs: the twigs hang downward under their own weight, giving a distinctly pendulous crown.
- **Lower branches:** *Estimate:* persist low on the bole; the reference habit photograph shows branches beginning low on a 16 m stem.
- **Crown layered?** *Estimate:* no strong layering — a loose, evenly distributed, open crown.

## Trunk

- **Form:** straight, slender. The reference habit photograph shows the tree **often dividing into 2–3 leaning stems from the base**; FRPS describes only 乔木 ("tree") and does not mention multi-stemming, so treat multi-stem as an **estimate from the photographs**.
- **Bark: reddish-brown (红褐色)**, and on older stems it separates into **papery layers that peel away in horizontal strips and flakes**, exposing a cinnamon/chestnut inner bark. Reference image `02` shows this at close range with snow-mountain background.
- **Not spiny.** Smooth young stems.

## Foliage

- **Leaf type:** simple, alternate.
- **Leaf size: 4–12 cm long × 2.5–5.5 cm wide.**
- **Shape: lanceolate or ovate-lanceolate** (披针形或卵状披针形) — in the photographs it reads mostly ovate-lanceolate, broadest below the middle.
- **Apex: acuminate to caudate-acuminate** (渐尖至尾状渐尖) — drawn out into a long tail-like point. This is a strong recognition feature at close range.
- **Base: cuneate, broadly cuneate, or rounded, rarely slightly cordate** (楔形、宽楔形或圆形，少有微心形).
- **Margin: irregular double serration with inward-curving bristle-like teeth** (内弯的刺毛状的不规则重锯齿). Doubly serrate with the secondary teeth pointing inward — not a simple saw-tooth.
- **Texture: thick papery (厚纸质)** and stiff.
- **Venation: 10–13 pairs of lateral veins.**
- **Petiole: 1.5–3(–4) cm**, densely covered with long soft hairs and glandular dots.
- **Indumentum:** upper surface glabrous; lower surface sparsely hairy along the veins and **densely bearded in the vein axils**, otherwise glabrous, and **densely covered with resin dots** (密生腺点) — the underside is glittery with resin glands.
- **Colour:** *The flora gives no colour.* *Estimate:* bright mid-green, with new growth a distinctly paler yellow-green; autumning to yellow-brown before fall.
- **Deciduous:** yes.

## Reproductive

- **Infructescences:** **long cylindrical**, **(2–)3–5 arranged in a raceme**, **5–10 cm long × 4–6 mm across**.
- **Raceme peduncle (总梗): 5–10 mm**; **pedicel (序梗): 2–3 mm**; both densely covered with **yellow long soft hairs** — the inflorescence stalks are conspicuously yellow-feltted.
- **Bracts (果苞): very small, about 3 mm**, back densely short-hairy, margin fringed with cilia, base cuneate, upper part split into **3 lobes** of which the **lateral lobes are poorly developed and ear-like** while the **median lobe is oblong with a blunt apex**.
- **Nutlets:** obovate, **1.5–2 mm**, sparsely short-hairy on the back; the **membranous wing is mostly exserted beyond the bract and is twice the width of the nut**.
- **Pollen catkins:** the photographs show slender cylindrical male catkins held erect to ascending at the shoot tips in leaf, ripening to tan.
- **Season:** not stated by FRPS for this species. *Estimate from the photographs:* pollen with leaf-flush in spring, infructescences ripening through summer into autumn.

## The five facts that let you recognise this tree from 200 m

1. **Pliability.** A slim 16 m tree with a loose, airy, slightly weeping crown of thin hanging twigs — it reads as a light, see-through mass, not a solid dark crown. This is the single most obvious character.
2. **Pale-and-rust vertical striping on the bole.** Papery bark peeling in horizontal bands to a cinnamon underlayer gives a mottled pale-and-brown column that is neither chalk-white nor smooth grey.
3. **A washed-out, yellow-green flush.** The new canopy is much paler than the deep green of the evergreen broadleaves beside it, so in spring the birch stands out as a light-green tree among dark ones.
4. **Several slim leaning stems, not one heavy bole.** The habit photograph shows 2–3 stems from the base, each narrow, which makes the tree look clumped and slender even in leaf.
5. **Long pendulous catkins.** Slender catkins dangling from the shoots — most obvious in leaf-flush and at fruit, giving the crown a fringe of hanging threads.

**Discarded, and why.** Four of the ten PPBC candidates were the filler frames PPBC serves for species it holds no photographs of (a pink *Ceiba*, a white water-lily, a yellow forest herb, a second pink *Ceiba*) — identified by being byte-identical to the same four files returned for 木棉 and *Bombax malabaricum*. Two further PPBC candidates were duplicates of kept files. Of the GBIF set, the four **Thailand** records show a narrow-leaved tropical broadleaf in a garden landscape that is plainly not *Betula alnoides* — that species does not occur in Thailand — and were discarded; a second India frame was a near-duplicate of `01-habit`.

**Provenance caveat.** `01-habit` and `02-bark` are Himalayan (India) trees from GBIF/iNaturalist, whereas the species' Chinese range is southern Yunnan and Hainan. Indian Himalayan *Betula* is commonly confused with *Betula utilis*, and these frames are observer-identified rather than expert-identified. They are used here for **growth form and bark texture**, where the two species are broadly comparable; the leaf, catkin and fruit characters come from the Chinese PPBC frames, which are expert-identified.
