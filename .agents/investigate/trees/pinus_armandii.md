## Site

- **Altitude: 1000–3300 m.** Source: FOC habitat field via
  `https://www.iplant.cn/ashx/getfoc.ashx?key=Pinus%20armandii`
  → "Mountains, river basins; 1000-3300 m."
- **Provinces (FOC list, verbatim):** S Gansu, C and NW Guizhou, Hainan, SW Henan, W Hubei,
  S Shaanxi, S Shanxi, Sichuan, C Taiwan, SE Xizang, Yunnan; also N Myanmar.
  *Note the unusual breadth — this is one of the most widely ranging Chinese pines, and the range
  runs from Gansu/Shaanxi in the north to Hainan and Taiwan in the south.*
- **Belt:** montane conifer forest, the 1000–3300 m band across moist mountain slopes and river
  basins. *("Vegetation belt it dominates" is my characterisation; FOC gives the altitude band and
  the landform only.)*
- **Grows with:** other montane conifers and broadleaved forest. *(Estimate/inference — FOC gives no
  community list; `10-site-forest.jpg` shows it forming a closed-canopy conifer stand in mountain
  forest, with shade-tolerant broadleaved trees beneath.)*

## Habit

- **Mature height / trunk diameter — FOC, exact:** "**Trees to 35 m tall; trunk to 1 m d.b.h.**"
- **Silhouette in one sentence:** a tall, straight, clean-boled pine with a broad, high, irregular to
  gently flattened crown of stiff, radiating, bottlebrush-like sprays, its limbs often contorted and
  carrying very large pendant cones.
- **Crown change with age:** **FOC states the crown as "conical or cylindric-pyramidal"** — i.e. the
  flora's own answer is a **conical/cylindric-pyramidal crown, retained into maturity**, not a
  flat-topped one. This is the opposite of *Pinus yunnanensis*, and is the cheapest way to tell the
  two apart in a forest. *Caveat from the photographs:* open-grown adults in `09-habit-second.jpg`
  broaden at the top and the outline becomes somewhat irregular, but the crown keeps a broadly
  pyramidal-to-cylindric envelope. **Modelling should keep a conical/cylindric-pyramidal envelope
  through life, with the apex rounding off in old open-grown trees.**

## Branching

- **Whorled or spiral:** **whorled**, and here it is *shown* rather than merely conventional — the
  first-order branches leave the trunk in a clean radial whorl in `03-bark.jpg`, the best single
  branching reference in this set. FOC does not state branch arrangement.
- **First-order branch attitude:** spreading and roughly **horizontal to slightly ascending**, with
  the branch itself markedly **sinuous/contorted** and the outer portions drooping under foliage
  weight. Visible in `03-bark.jpg` and `04-branch.jpg`. *Inferred from photographs; FOC gives no
  attitude.*
- **Lower branches:** lower branches are shed, giving a **clean trunk** — `01-habit.jpg` and
  `09-habit-second.jpg` both show a completely branch-free bole over much of the height.
- **Layered or tiered:** **yes** — the whorls read as discrete tiers, especially in `03-bark.jpg`,
  where the whorl structure of the primary branching is unmistakable.

## Trunk

- **Straight or contorted:** trunk **straight**; the *branches* are the contorted part, not the bole.
  See `03-bark.jpg` (straight stem, wildly sinuous limbs) and `01-habit.jpg`.
- **Base:** in `03-bark.jpg` the bole widens modestly into the first whorl, with a slight flare at
  the ground and no strong buttressing. *Inferred from photograph.*
- **Bark colour and texture — FOC, exact:** "bark **gray, fissured into square plates or shed**."
  So the bark breaks into distinctly **square-ish plates**, and the plates **shed** — it is a
  shedding, plated bark, not the small scaly bark of *P. yunnanensis*. `03-bark.jpg` shows the grey
  bark on the stem and the smoother grey bark of the young limbs.
- **Branchlets — FOC, exact:** "branchlets **green, gray-green, or -brown, glaucous, turning brown
  when dry, glabrous**" — the glaucous bloom is visible as the pale sheen on the young limbs in
  `04-branch.jpg`.
- **Winter buds — FOC, exact:** "winter buds **almost cylindric, slightly resinous**." (Contrast
  *P. yunnanensis*: buds not resinous. *P. armandii* is the resinous one.)

## Foliage

All figures from FOC via `https://www.iplant.cn/ashx/getfoc.ashx?key=Pinus%20armandii`:

- **Type:** needles in fascicles.
- **Number per fascicle: 5(-7)** — the definitive five-needle pine. Confirmed in `05-leaf.jpg`, where
  an isolated fascicle on a white background carries five long needles.
- **Length × width: 8–15 cm × 1–1.5 mm.**
- **Cross-section: triangular** — the needle is **angular and prickle-like in section**, unlike the
  flattened, sickle-shaped needles of *P. yunnanensis*. This, plus the threefold needle length, is
  why the crown reads as stiff and bristly rather than soft and drooping.
- **Vascular bundles: 1. Resin canals: 3(-7), median or 2 marginal.**
- **Terminal rosettes:** no — fascicles along the shoot.
- **Margin:** entire, no teeth.
- **Evergreen:** yes. Needle persistence **3–4 years** — *ESTIMATE; FOC states no retention period.*
- **Colour:** dark green, glaucous when young (see the glaucous branchlets above), ageing to a
  yellow-green. `05-leaf.jpg` shows the dark, glossy, slightly glaucous green of a fresh fascicle.

## Reproductive

All from FOC via the URL above:

- **Pollen cones:** "erect or drooping, slender or stout, cylindric or ovoid-ellipsoid" — no
  dimensions given. Visible in `08-flowers.jpg` as small erect cones clustered at the shoot apex
  beside a young female cone.
- **Seed cones:** **pedunculate on a stout 2–3 cm peduncle** — an unusually long, conspicuous stalk
  for a pine and clearly visible in `06-cone.jpg` and `07-cone-pendant-pair.jpg`. Green at first,
  maturing **yellow or brown-yellow**; **conical-cylindric**; **dehiscent at maturity**, shedding seeds.
  FOC gives no length figure — `06-cone.jpg` shows a cone of roughly forearm length and bulk.
  **ESTIMATE: 10–20 cm long**, estimated from the 2–3 cm peduncle and the needle scale in the same
  frame in `06-cone.jpg`.
- **Seed scales:** rhombic-obovate, **3–4 × 2.5–3 cm**; apophyses rhombic or triangular, **not ridged**,
  apex obtuse-rounded or acuminate, not or slightly recurved; **umbo not obvious**.
- **Seeds:** yellow-brown, dark brown or black, obovoid, **1–1.5 cm × 6–10 mm** — note
  **wingless** (or with a ridged abaxial margin, rarely shortly winged). The wingless seed is a
  strong genus-level character of this group.
- **Orientation and position:** the cones are **pendant**, hanging from the ends of the peduncle
  below the branch — `07-cone-pendant-pair.jpg` shows two of them hanging clear of the foliage, and
  `06-cone.jpg` shows the cone hanging below a whorl of fascicles. They sit near the ends of the
  shoots, not in a terminal whorl.
- **Season:** pollination **Apr–May**; seed maturity **Sep–Oct of the second year** — i.e. cones hang
  on the tree for **two seasons**, so a mature tree carries conspicuous cones through the year.

## The five facts that identify this tree from 200 m, as a silhouette

1. **Stiff, radiating, bottlebrush sprays of long needles**, distinctly coarser and more bristly than
   云南松's soft drooping sprays. The foliage texture is coarse, not wispy.
2. **A conical or cylindric-pyramidal crown that stays that way in maturity** — the top is pointed or
   columnar, not flattened. This is the single cleanest separation from 云南松.
3. **A clean, tall, branchless bole**, crown starting well above half the tree's height.
4. **Very large, heavy, pendant cones hanging below the branches**, in pairs or small clusters — at
   200 m they read as distinct pale-brown blobs under the crown. They persist two seasons, so they
   are visible most of the year.
5. **Grey bark that breaks into square plates and sheds**, and a branching pattern of strongly
   **sinuous, contorted horizontal limbs** emerging in clean radial whorls.
