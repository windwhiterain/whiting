# 沙棘 — Hippophae rhamnoides

*Measured 2026-10-18. One question: what does this shrub look like, and how does it grow?*

> **Read this first — it changes the model.** "沙棘" in Chinese horticulture means **subsp. sinensis**, the northern fruit crop from the Loess Plateau. **The Southwest-China plant is subsp. yunnanensis**, and FRPS separates the two on exactly the leaf characters a modeller would use. In particular:
> - **subsp. yunnanensis leaves are ALTERNATE** and **widest at the base** (常为圆形或有时楔形); subsp. sinensis leaves are **usually nearly opposite** and narrow-lanceolate.
> - **The underside of subsp. yunnanensis is GREY-BROWN with abundant large rust-coloured scales, NOT silver-white.** The famous silver-white underside (叶背银白色) belongs to subsp. sinensis, turkestanica and mongolica.
> So "the silver-leaved dry-valley shrub" describes the *northern* subspecies. **Modelling a bright silver underside for the Yunnan/Sichuan plant would be wrong.**

## Identity

| | |
|---|---|
| Chinese name | 沙棘 |
| Accepted binomial | **Hippophae rhamnoides L.**, Sp. Pl. 1023. 1753 |
| Family | **Elaeagnaceae** (GBIF key 3039285, ACCEPTED) |
| Infraspecific structure | Rousi (1971, Turku) divided the species into **9 subspecies, 5 of them in China**. **The nominate subsp. rhamnoides does not occur in China.** |
| Neotype | Linnaeus' type was never found; Rousi designated a neotype from the **Uppsala Botanical Garden, Sweden**, concluding Linnaeus' plant was the north-European coastal species. That is why Chinese plants were long called *H. rhamnoides* sensu stricto. |
| The SW-China subspecies | **subsp. yunnanensis Rousi**, Ann. Bot. Fennici 8: 213. 1971 — type from **云南中甸** (Zhongdian / Shangri-La) |
| Rejected | var. procera Rehd. — Rousi judged the white stellate tomentum not diagnostic and merged it into subsp. sinensis |

## Site

- **Altitude: 2200–3700 m** for subsp. yunnanensis — a high, cold, dry belt.
- **Ground:** **dry river-valley sand, gravel ground, mountain slopes in dense forest through to alpine grassland** (干涸河谷沙地、石砾地或山坡密林中至高山草地). This is the dry-valley shrub role the project is after.
- **Provinces:** 四川宝兴 (Baoxing), south of 康定 (Kangding), northwestern Yunnan, and Tibet east of Lhasa. Also Bhutan, northern Myanmar.

## Habit

- **Size:** FRPS gives no height for subsp. yunnanensis. For the near-identical subsp. sinensis it is **1–5 m, reaching 18 m in high-mountain gullies** (高山沟谷可达18米), deciduous shrub or small tree.
- **Growth form:** **multi-stemmed clump of arching, spine-tipped shoots** — a classic dry-valley thorny shrub.
- **Spines:** numerous and stout, **terminal or lateral** (棘刺较多，粗壮，顶生或侧生) — *stated for subsp. sinensis; the Yunnan subspecies is described as very close.*
- **Silhouette, one sentence:** a dense, grey-green, twiggy mound several metres across whose arching shoot tips are armed with stout spines and whose whole surface carries a rusty-silvery sheen from the peltate scales.

## Branching

- **Shoot surface:** young shoots brownish-green, **densely covered with silvery-white scales tinged brown, or sometimes with white stellate tomentum**; **older branches grey-black and rough**.
- **Bud size:** **large**, golden-yellow or rust-coloured.
- **Spines:** see Habit.
- **Branch colour:** subsp. sinensis shoots brownish-green → grey-black with age; subsp. gyantsensis shoots grey or brown and slender with **shorter internodes**.
- **Phyllotaxy — the key modelling fact:** **subsp. yunnanensis leaves are ALTERNATE** (叶互生); subsp. sinensis leaves are **usually nearly opposite** (通常近对生). FOC's key gives the same split.
- **Lower branches:** *Estimate:* the whole shoot system is deciduous and the shrub resprouts from the base, so model the canopy as many arching stems from a common base.

## Trunk

- Multi-stemmed; each stem woody, grey-black and rough with age. *FRPS gives no bark description for this subspecies.*

## Foliage

- **Leaf type:** simple.
- **Size (subsp. yunnanensis):** FRPS does not restate the blade dimensions for this subspecies; it is described as very close to subsp. sinensis, whose leaves are **3–8 cm long × 0.4–1.0(1.3) cm wide**. *Estimate:* use that range for subsp. yunnanensis, marked as an estimate.
- **Shape:** narrow-lanceolate to oblong-lanceolate, **widest at the base**, the base **often rounded, sometimes cuneate**, both ends blunt.
- **Arrangement:** **alternate**; **spirally arranged flower buds** (FOC's distinguishing character for subsp. yunnanensis).
- **Colour — the important one:** **upper surface green; lower surface GREY-BROWN, bearing plentiful, relatively large rust-coloured scales** (下面灰褐色，具较多而较大的锈色鳞片). FOC phrases it "abaxially dirty gray with many rust-colored peltate hairs". **Not silver-white.**
- **Indumentum:** the whole leaf surface is scaled; the scales are the reason the plant reads silvery or rusty at distance.
- **Petiole:** extremely short, virtually absent or 1–1.5 mm.

## Reproductive

- **Fruit: globose, 5–7 mm in diameter** for subsp. yunnanensis (4–6 mm for subsp. sinensis).
- **Fruit stalk: 1–2 mm** for subsp. yunnanensis.
- **Seed: broadly ellipsoid to ovoid, slightly flattened, usually 3–4 mm long.**
- **Flowers:** April; **fruit ripens August–September** for subsp. yunnanensis (subsp. sinensis flowers April–May, fruits September–October).
- **Modelling note:** the dense masses of orange berries are the plant's main visual event and are far more conspicuous than the flowers.

## The five facts that let you recognise this plant from 200 m

1. **A grey-green, twiggy, rounded mound in dry country.** It sits in arid river valleys and gravel slopes as a low dense thicket, not as a forest tree — a big blocky grey-green mass against bare ground.
2. **A rusty-silver sheen, not a bright silver.** The leaves are grey-brown beneath with heavy rust-coloured scaling, so the bush reads dusty olive-rust from a distance. (A bright silver-white plant in the same habitat is a *different* subspecies.)
3. **Long, dense, upright spines bristling from every shoot tip.** The twigs end in a fine forest of stiff points, which gives the crown a fuzzy, bristly edge.
4. **Strongly golden-yellow young stems and buds.** The new growth and the large buds are golden yellow to rust, brighter than the foliage.
5. **Walls of orange berries in late summer.** Globose orange fruits crowd the shoot tips from August–September, turning the upper half of the bush into a visible orange haze — the single feature to get right for the fruit season.
