## Site

- **Altitude: 400–3100 m.** Source: FOC habitat field, via
  `https://www.iplant.cn/ashx/getfoc.ashx?key=Pinus%20yunnanensis`
  → "Mountains, river basins, dry and sunny slopes; 400-3100 m."
- **Provinces:** Guangxi, Guizhou, SW Sichuan, SE Xizang (Tibet), Yunnan. Source: same FOC field.
- **Belt:** the dry, sunny, strongly seasonal slopes of the lower and middle mountains. FOC's
  wording "dry and sunny slopes" is the operative fact — this is a light- and drought-demanding pine
  of the lower and middle mountain slopes, not a subalpine tree.
  *("Vegetation belt it dominates" is my characterisation, not FOC's; FOC supplies the altitude band
  and the aspect/moisture preference only.)*
- **Grows with:** other pines on the same sunny slopes, evergreen broadleaved forest below it, and
  mixed conifer–broadleaf forest above. *(Estimate/inference — FOC gives no community list; inferred
  from the altitude band and from `10-crown-cones-sky.jpg`, which shows it on open steep ground.)*

## Habit

- **Mature height / trunk diameter:** Flora of China does **not** give a height or diameter figure
  for this species — it opens with "Trees or rarely shrubs" and gives no size.
  **ESTIMATE: 25–30 m tall, trunk to roughly 0.8 m d.b.h.** Estimated from (a) the trunk-to-crown
  proportion visible in `01-habit.jpg` and `09-habit-plantation.jpg` (clean bole of roughly half the
  total height; trunk diameter roughly 1/40 of height) and (b) general scaling of the Yunnan plateau
  pines. **Treat these two numbers as estimates, not as flora data.**
- **Silhouette in one sentence:** a tall, straight, clean-boled pine with a broad, flat-topped or
  irregular mature crown of very long, lax, drooping, grey-green needle sprays, its limbs visibly
  studded with clusters of pale persistent cones.
- **Crown change with age — the requested answer:** **juvenile conical, mature flat-topped /
  irregular.** This is read from the photographs, not from FOC (FOC is silent on crown shape here):
  the younger trees in `09-habit-plantation.jpg` show a narrow conical spire with clearly separated
  whorl tiers, while `01-habit.jpg` shows the mature form — a broad, flattish, irregular crown with
  upswept branch ends and a ragged, non-conical outline. **Modelling should interpolate
  conical → flat-topped.**

## Branching

- **Whorled or spiral:** first-order branches are **whorled**. FOC does not state branch arrangement
  for this species; whorled is the standard and visibly tiered arrangement in *Pinus*, and the tiers
  are plainly visible in `09-habit-plantation.jpg`. *Flagged as an inference, not an FOC quotation.*
- **First-order branch attitude:** spreading, roughly horizontal to ascending, turning up at the tips
  in mature trees. *Inferred from `01-habit.jpg` and `04-branch.jpg`; FOC gives no attitude.*
- **Lower branches:** the lower branches die and are shed, leaving a **clean trunk** over the lower
  third to half of the tree. Unambiguous in `01-habit.jpg` and `09-habit-plantation.jpg`, where the
  bole is completely branch-free for many metres.
- **Layered or tiered:** **yes in the juvenile** — the whorls form discrete tiers with clear gaps
  (`09-habit-plantation.jpg`). In the mature crown the tiers remain traceable but are blurred by the
  long drooping needle sprays.

## Trunk

- **Straight or contorted:** **straight.** `01-habit.jpg` shows a plumb bole; the leaning habit seen
  at exposed sites is a site effect, not the species default.
- **Base:** not strongly buttressed in the photographs; the bole meets the ground cleanly with at
  most a slight flare. *Inferred from `01-habit.jpg`.*
- **Bark colour and texture — FOC, exact:** "bark **brown-gray, longitudinally fissured, scaly**",
  from `https://www.iplant.cn/ashx/getfoc.ashx?key=Pinus%20yunnanensis`.
  It flakes and plates: the surface breaks into small scaly plates elongated along the trunk axis and
  shedding from the fissures. `03-bark.jpg` shows this directly — grey-brown bark broken into
  irregular, roughly rectangular scaly plates with dark fissures between them.
- **Branchlets — FOC, exact:** "branchlets **reddish brown, thick, glabrous**."
- **Winter buds — FOC, exact:** "winter buds **red-brown, ovoid, large, not resinous**" — the absence
  of resin is a useful separation from *Pinus armandii*.

## Foliage

All figures from FOC via `https://www.iplant.cn/ashx/getfoc.ashx?key=Pinus%20yunnanensis`:

- **Type:** needles in fascicles on short shoots, from a persistent basal sheath —
  "base with **persistent sheath**".
- **Number per fascicle: (2 or) 3.** Confirmed visually in `05-leaf.jpg`, where the isolated fascicles
  on the black background carry three needles each.
- **Length × width: 7–30 cm × 1–1.2 mm.** This extreme length range is the single most diagnostic
  number in this note: 30 cm is a very long pine needle.
- **Cross-section:** "flabellate-triangular or semiorbicular" — the needle is **flattened and
  slightly sickle-curved**, not round. This is what makes the foliage read as soft and drooping
  rather than stiff.
- **Stomata:** "stomatal lines present on **all** surfaces" (amphistomatic) — hence the pale, silvery
  cast, visible in `04-branch.jpg` and `10-crown-cones-sky.jpg`.
- **Resin canals:** 4 or 5, median and marginal.
- **Terminal rosettes:** no. Foliage is in fascicles along the shoot, not in terminal rosettes.
- **Margin:** entire — needles have no teeth.
- **Evergreen:** yes. Needle persistence **2–3 years** — *this figure is an ESTIMATE; FOC does not
  state a retention period.* `07-cone-persistent-old.jpg`, where old brown cones and fresh foliage
  coexist on one branch, shows multi-year retention of foliage.
- **Colour:** grey-green to yellow-green, glaucous; stomatal lines on both surfaces give a distinctly
  paler, silvery-green cast than a typical 2-needle pine.

## Reproductive

All from FOC via the URL above:

- **Pollen cones:** not separately described in the FOC entry. Visible in `08-flowers.jpg` as small
  clustered structures near the shoot apex.
- **Seed cones: 5–11 cm long**, conical-ovoid, **shortly pedunculate**, green at first, maturing to
  brown or chestnut brown. **Dehiscent or indehiscent at maturity** — the species is variable and can
  hold its cones closed.
- **Seed scales:** oblong-ellipsoid, ca. **3 × 1.5 cm**; apophyses usually swollen and cross-keeled,
  rarely recurved; umbo slightly sunken or slightly protruded, ending in a **minute prickle**.
- **Seeds:** brown, slightly appressed, nearly ovoid to obovoid, **4–5 mm**; wing **1.2–1.4 cm**.
- **Orientation and position:** the cones sit **subterminal / lateral on the shoots**, not as a whorl
  at the shoot tip, and hang once mature — see `07-cone-persistent-old.jpg`, where old brown cones hang
  beneath the branch. **Critically for silhouette: old cones persist on the branches for years**, so a
  mature tree is visibly studded with cone clusters.
- **Season:** pollination **Apr–May**; seed maturity **October** of the same year.

## The five facts that identify this tree from 200 m, as a silhouette

1. **Very long, lax, drooping, pale grey-green needle sprays** — the crown reads soft, shaggy and
   wispy, never as the stiff bristle of a 2-needle pine. This alone separates it from every other pine
   of the region at distance.
2. **Broad, flat-topped or irregular mature crown**, not a spire; the outline is ragged, with upswept
   branch ends rather than a clean conical taper. (Juveniles *are* conical, so silhouette must be a
   function of age.)
3. **A clean, long, branchless bole** — the crown starts at roughly half the tree's height, giving a
   tall bare trunk under a floating green mass.
4. **Pale cone clusters studding the crown**, because old cones stay attached for years. At 200 m
   these read as light speckles across the darker foliage.
5. **Grey-brown scaly bark on a straight bole**, with the crown **narrow relative to height in young
   trees and broad relative to height in old ones** — the crown widens faster than the trunk lengthens.
