## Site

- **Altitude:** **700–2600 m**.
- **Provinces:** Yunnan; western and southwestern Guizhou; southwestern Sichuan, along the Anning River (安宁河) valley and westward to the upper Dadu River (大渡河).
- **Vegetation belt:** dry warm-temperate montane conifer forest. FRPS: 常混生于云南松林中或组成小片纯林 — mixed into *Pinus yunnanensis* forest, or forming small pure stands. Also planted as 人工林.
- **Grows with:** *Pinus yunnanensis* is the named associate — the two share the same montane belt, and a *Keteleeria* reads as the darker, glossier, more massive tree among the pines.

## Habit

- **Mature height:** to **40 m** (乔木，高达40米).
- **Trunk diameter:** **DBH to 1 m** (胸径可达1米). The flora gives this, unusually — most species here have no diameter at all.
- **Silhouette, one sentence:** a very tall, straight, massive conifer whose heavy whorled limbs carry a broad, dense, dark cone-shaped crown that rounds slightly toward a drawn-out apex.
- **Branches** are 较粗，开展 — noticeably thick for their conifer genus, and held out (开展), not merely ascending.
- **Crown change with age:** *Estimate:* conical and spire-like in the first third of life; with age the crown widens relative to height, the lowest whorls die back, and the outline becomes a broad cone with a bare lower bole.

## Branching

- **Phyllotaxy:** not stated by FRPS. **Estimate:** whorled, 3–5 branches per whorl, as is typical of the genus.
- **First-order branch angle:** **spreading, near-horizontal to ascending** — FRPS 开展 and the reference photographs show limbs standing well out from the trunk. *Estimate 45–75° from vertical, from the photographs.*
- **Branch character:** branches are 较粗 (rather thick) — this tree carries visibly heavier limbs than a *Pinus* of the same height.
- **Lower branches:** *Estimate:* persist on mature trees except the lowest whorls, so the crown stays tiered for most of the trunk.
- **Crown layered?** *Estimate:* yes — clearly tiered, each whorl reading as a separate horizontal plate, which is why the crown looks "coarse" rather than smooth.

## Trunk

- **Form:** straight, cylindrical, erect; massive for its height class.
- **Bark:** **rough, dark grey-brown (暗灰褐色), with irregular deep longitudinal fissures, and it sheds in blocks or plates (成块状脱落)**. This is a blocky, chunky-fissured bark — not the thin papery bark of the birch it grows beside.
- **Young branches:** one-year-old branches dry to **pink or pale brownish-red (粉红色或淡褐红色)** and are usually hairy; two- and three-year-old branches are glabrous, grey-brown / yellow-brown / brown, and their bark splits into **thin flakes** (枝皮裂成薄片). The colour change with age is a usable detail.

## Foliage

- **Leaf type:** **needle-like (条形)**, and crucially **arranged in two ranks (排列成两列) on lateral shoots** — flattened into a single plane, not radially star-like. This is visible at a distance as a dark, flat, fern-like frond.
- **Needle size:** **2–6.5 cm long × 2–3(3.5) mm wide.**
- **Apex:** usually a **slightly raised blunt point** (微凸起的钝尖头); **on young trees and on sprout shoots the needle ends in a slightly acute, spine-like long point** (微急尖的刺状长尖头). That spiny young-tree needle is a useful juvenile-form flag.
- **Base:** cuneate, narrowing gradually into a short petiole.
- **Colour:** upper surface **glossy green (光绿色)**; beneath, along the midrib, **14–19 stomatal lines on each side**, which read as two pale white bands. The upper surface carries only 2–10, rarely none.
- **Evergreen:** the FRPS entry for this species does not say so explicitly; *Keteleeria* is an evergreen conifer and the reference photographs are of fully foliated trees. Recorded here as a genus-level fact, not a quotation.

## Reproductive

- **Cones:** **cylindrical (球果圆柱形), 9–20 cm long × 4–6.5 cm in diameter** — very large for the genus, and roughly three times as long as wide.
- **Seed scales:** ovate to rhombic / obliquely rhombic-ovate, **3–4 cm × 2.5–3 cm**, with the **upper part recurved outward (上部向外反曲)** and the **margin bearing obvious small teeth (明显的细小缺齿)**.
- **Bracts:** narrow in the middle, gradually widening below, nearly rounded above, apex **obscurely three-lobed** with the middle lobe the obvious one.
- **Seed:** winged, the wing **widest in its lower-middle and narrowing upward (种翅中下部较宽，上部渐窄)**.
- **Cone orientation:** the cones stand **erect** on the branches. FRPS does not state this in words, but the two cone photographs kept below (05, 02) show them standing upright along the crown, and the description of a long cylindrical cone with scale tips recurved outward is consistent with that.
- **Season:** pollen April–May; **seeds mature in October**.

## The five facts that let you recognise this tree from 200 m

1. **The erect cones.** Big upright cylinders, 9–20 cm, studded all over the outer crown. Against the sky the crown reads as a coarse, knobbly, "corn-cob" texture. Nothing else in that forest does this, and it is the first thing to look for.
2. **Height and straightness.** A clean, straight, unbroken bole carrying the crown high — a 40 m vertical line in a forest otherwise made of crooked multi-stemmed broadleaves.
3. **A broad, dense, dark, glossy crown.** Noticeably darker and more lustrous than the *Pinus yunnanensis* around it, with a conical outline that rounds toward a drawn-out apex.
4. **Heavy whorled limbs held well out.** Thick branches standing nearly horizontal in tiers, giving the crown a coarse, layered, plate-by-plate texture rather than a smooth surface.
5. **A coarse, blocky, dark bole.** Dark grey-brown bark in irregular deep longitudinal fissures, flaking off in plates — visually much chunkier than the smooth grey of the birches that colonise beside it.
