# 云南红豆杉 — Taxus yunnanensis (the brief's "Taxus wallichiana var. yunnanensis")

*Measured 2026-10-18. One question: what does this tree look like, and how does it grow?*

> **Naming correction — read before modelling.** Neither source in this project uses the name *Taxus wallichiana var. yunnanensis*:
> - **中国植物志 (FRPS)** treats 云南红豆杉 as a **separate species**, ***Taxus yunnanensis* Cheng et L. K. Fu** (Acta Phytotax. Sin. 13(4): 87. 1975). FRPS says explicitly that it was **formerly misidentified as *T. wallichiana*** (曾误定为西藏红豆杉 *T. wallichiana* Zucc.) and lists the characters that separate it.
> - **GBIF** returns **no record at all** for `Taxus wallichiana var. yunnanensis` (an empty result set), and lists *Taxus yunnanensis* W.C.Cheng & L.K.Fu as a **SYNONYM** (key 5284552) — i.e. GBIF folds it into *T. wallichiana* Zucc.
>
> So the two authorities disagree in *opposite* directions, and the brief's name is used by neither. **This note is written to the FRPS species account**, because FRPS is the source that gives the measurements. The file keeps the brief's slug so the wave stays traceable.

## Site

- **Altitude: 2000–3500 m**, a high-montane belt.
- **Provinces:** northwestern and western Yunnan (镇康 Zhengkang, 景东 Jingdong); southwestern Sichuan; southeastern Tibet. Also Bhutan and northern Myanmar.
- **Grows with:** FRPS records it in **dense forest on slopes through to alpine grassland** (山坡密林中至高山草地), and on **dry river-valley sand and gravel ground** (干涸河谷沙地、石砾地).
- **Wood:** heartwood and sapwood clearly distinct, even grain, fine structure, hard, tough, little cracking after drying. Used for building, bridges, furniture, vehicles; suitable for afforestation.

## Habit

- **Mature height: to 20 m. DBH to 1 m.**
- **Main branches spreading (大枝开展).**
- **Silhouette, one sentence:** a medium-sized, dark, dense conifer with a broad irregular crown of flat, horizontal sprays of long falcate needles and thin pendulous branchlets.
- **Crown change with age:** *Estimate:* a narrow spire when young, opening into a broad, irregular, layered crown; the branchlets stay pendulous.

## Branching

- **Phyllotaxy:** leaves **arranged rather sparsely (排列较疏) in two rows** (列成两列). FOC's account of the species group adds that leafy branchlets are **± flat in the living state, 3–9 × 1.5–6 cm in outline**, with leaves borne at **(50–)60–90° to the branchlet axis** — i.e. spreading nearly at right angles, not swept forward along the shoot. That is what makes the sprays look flat and fern-like.
- **Branch colour by year:** 1-year green, turning **golden yellow-green or yellow-green** in autumn/after drying; 2-year pale brown, brown or yellow-brown; 3- and 4-year dark brown. *This strong golden-yellow first-year colour is a usable seasonal signal.*
- **Winter buds:** golden yellow-green, **bud scales narrow**, apex acuminate, back with longitudinal ridges, **deciduous or partly persistent at the base of the branchlet**.
- **Lower branches:** *Estimate:* persist for a good part of the trunk; yews generally keep foliage low.

## Trunk

- **Form:** straight, cylindrical.
- **Bark: grey-brown, grey-purple or pale purple-brown, splitting and falling off in thin scaly plates** (灰褐色、灰紫色或淡紫褐色，裂成鳞状薄片脱落).

## Foliage

- **Leaf type:** needles.
- **Size: 1.5–4.7 cm long (usually 2–3 cm) × 2–3 mm wide.**
- **Texture: thin and flexible (质地薄而柔)** — this is one of the two characters that separates it from *T. wallichiana*, whose leaves are notably **thicker and stiffer**.
- **Shape: linear-lanceolate or lanceolate-linear, and characteristically **falcate — bent like a sickle** (常呈弯镰状)**. This recurvature is the single most useful modelling cue for the species.
- **Arrangement:** **rather sparse, in two rows** — note that *T. wallichiana* has them **denser and in irregular overlapping two rows** (较密地排列成彼此重叠的不规则两列), so the Yunnan yew's sprays look more open.
- **Apex: gradually acuminate or slightly acute, tapering to a point**; the blade **narrows gradually toward the tip**.
- **Base: oblique (基部偏歪)** — the two sides of the blade are asymmetric at the base, another separating character.
- **Margin: revolute downward or recurved (边缘向下反卷或反曲), obvious in dried leaves.**
- **Colour:** **upper surface dark green or green and glossy**; **lower surface paler**, midrib slightly raised, with **a pale yellow-green stomatal band on each side** of the midrib. Both the midrib band and the stomatal bands are densely and evenly covered with tiny **keratinous papillae**. **Dried leaves darken, often to dark green.**
- **Evergreen:** yes.

## Reproductive

- **Male cones (雄球花): pale brownish-yellow, 5–6 mm long × about 3 mm across**, with **9–11 stamens**, each stamen bearing **5 anthers**.
- **Aril: fleshy cup-shaped, bright RED when ripe (成熟时假种皮红色)** — the only red feature on the tree, and the reason the genus is worth noticing in fruit.
- **Seed: ovoid, about 5 mm long × 4 mm across, slightly flattened, usually narrowing above, with blunt ridges on the two sides, a small point at the apex, and an elliptic hilum.**
- **Season:** flowers **April**; fruit **August–September**.
- *Compared with* T. wallichiana *— whose seed is columnar-oblong, about 6.5 × 4.5–5 mm — the Yunnan yew's seed is ovoid and smaller at about 5 × 4 mm.*

## The five facts that let you recognise this tree from 200 m

1. **A dense, dark, broad-crowned evergreen against bare rock.** Unlike the spire-shaped *Tsuga* and *Keteleeria* of the same altitude, this yew's crown is **irregular and spreading**, filling a rounded mass rather than a cone.
2. **Long, pale, sickle-curved needles.** The foliage reads as a soft, slightly silvery-grey, weeping texture because the blades recurve and their margins roll under — quite unlike the flat, two-ranked dark plates of a hemlock or fir needle.
3. **Shoots that go golden yellow-green.** The young branchlets are conspicuously lighter — gold-green rather than dark green — so in leaf the tree carries a two-tone, yellow-flecked look.
4. **Thin, drooping, flat sprays.** The branchlets hang in flattened, feather-like planes with the needles standing out nearly at right angles, giving a loose, layered, pendulous crown outline.
5. **Red cups in autumn.** After the April flowering, the branch tips carry **bright red fleshy arils** on small dark seeds — scattered red beads on a dark green tree, the single most reliable seasonal marker from a distance.
