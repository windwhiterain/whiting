# 云南铁杉 — Tsuga dumosa

*Measured 2026-10-18. One question: what does this tree look like, and how does it grow?*

> **Naming note.** FRPS **merges** *Tsuga yunnanensis* (Franch.) Pritz. into *T. dumosa*, judging the supposed differences (branchlet colour, hair density, leaf shape, marginal teeth, cone and scale characters) to be mere variation (故予合并). Anyone modelling "Yunnan hemlock" should use the numbers below, which subsume that name.

## Site

- **Altitude: 2300–3500 m**, a high-montane belt. Often forms **pure stands (单纯林)** or mixed forest with other conifers.
- **Provinces:** southern Tibet; Yunnan (northwest, northeast, and Jingdong in the west); southwestern Sichuan (Dadu, upper Minjiang at Wenchuan/Lixian, Qingyi and Mabian valleys).
- **Grows with:** other montane conifers in mixed stands.
- **What it wants** (FRPS): plentiful rainfall, cool climate, comparatively high year-round temperature, frequent cloud/fog, high relative humidity, acidic soil, good drainage.
- **Wood:** straight-grained, fine, even, hard, water-resistant; used for building, aircraft, furniture, boats, mine props and wood-fibre industry.

## Habit

- **Mature height: to 40 m. DBH to 2.7 m** — one of the heaviest trunks in this whole list.
- **Silhouette, one sentence:** a very tall, massive hemlock with a dense, narrow **conical (尖塔形) spire** whose main limbs spread or droop slightly and whose branch tips hang down, giving the crown a slightly weeping, soft outline rather than the stiff symmetry of a fir.
- **Crown change with age:** *Estimate:* narrow spire when young, widening with age while the leader persists; the crown stays dense (浓密) and conical throughout.

## Branching

- **Main branches spreading or slightly drooping (大枝开展或微下垂); branch tips drooping (枝稍下垂).**
- **Branchlet colour by year:** 1-year yellow-brown / pale reddish-brown / pale brown, **hairy in the grooves or densely short-hairy**; 2- and 3-year pale brown, pale grey-brown or dark grey. Ridged and grooved, brown-lanate (FOC).
- **Phyllotaxy:** not stated; **estimate:** alternate, spirally arranged, but the needles are **swept forward along the shoot (pectinately arranged, FOC)**, lying in two ranks — this is what gives hemlocks their flat, fern-like sprays.
- **Lower branches:** *Estimate:* shed from the base with age, leaving a clean bole.

## Trunk

- **Form:** straight, cylindrical, very massive — **2.7 m DBH at 40 m** is a stout, pillar-like bole.
- **Bark: thick, rough, brownish-grey or dark grey-brown, longitudinally fissured and flaking off in flakes/plates** (厚，粗糙，褐灰色或暗灰褐色，纵裂成片状脱落).

## Foliage

- **Leaf type:** needles, **1–2.4 cm long (rarely to 3.5 cm) × 1.5–3 mm wide**, linear, rarely narrowing above to linear-lanceolate, **arranged in two rows** and combed forward.
- **Apex: obtuse-acute to obtuse, not notched, rarely slightly emarginate.**
- **Margin: finely serrate or entire** — the fine teeth usually sit on the **upper-middle part of the margin** only, rarely reaching lower.
- **Surfaces:** **upper glossy green, grooved adaxially**; **lower with two white stomatal bands**. One layer of hypodermis below the midrib and at the two ends in cross-section.
- **Evergreen:** yes.

## Reproductive

- **Cones: ovoid or narrowly ovoid, 1.5–3 cm long × 1–2 cm across**, light green, ripening **light brown**.
- **Seed scales:** oblong, obovate-oblong or long-ovoid, **1–1.4 × 0.7–1.2 cm**; distal margin thin and slightly recurved; base only slightly convex.
- **Bracts:** cuneate-rhombic, margin denticulate, **apex 2-lobed**.
- **Seeds:** obliquely ovoid or narrowly ovoid, **8–12 mm including the wing**.
- **Season:** pollination **April–May**; cones mature **October–November**.
- **Cones hang** — *estimate,* from the small ovoid cone and the drooping-branch habit; unlike *Keteleeria*, no part of this species is erect-coned.

## The five facts that let you recognise this tree from 200 m

1. **A dense, narrow, sharply conical spire.** The crown is a tight dark-green cone to a fine point — the classic spruce/hemlock tower, and much narrower than the *Keteleeria* or *Machilus* beside it.
2. **Enormous girth for the height.** A 40 m tree with a DBH up to **2.7 m** gives a bole that is visibly thick, pillar-like and massive, not slender.
3. **Drooping branch tips.** The limbs spread but the ends hang, so the crown outline is softly ragged and weeping along its lower edge instead of ending in sharp horizontal plates.
4. **Very dark, dense, fine-textured foliage** in the high-altitude cloud forest, reading as a near-solid dark mass rather than the lighter, glossier canopy of a broadleaf.
5. **A coarse, thick, blocky grey-brown bark** on the lower bole, flaking in plates — much heavier than the thin bark of the birch that pioneers the same ground.
