## Site

- **Altitude: 900–1000 m** — FRPS gives one figure for the species, a low-montane belt.
- **Habitat:** mountain forest (山地林中).
- **Provinces:** 陕西(镇巴), 安徽(歙县、休宁、舒城、岳西、潜山、霍山), 浙江(龙泉、遂昌、松阳), 江西(庐山), 福建(武夷山), 湖北(房县、巴东、建始、利川), 湖南(桑植、新宁), 广西(融水、临桂、龙胜、兴安、资源、灌阳、华江), 四川(万源、万县、秀山、南川、叙永、古蔺、锡连), 贵州(绥阳、息峰、黎平), 云南(彝良、大关、富宁、金平、麻栗坡); 台湾有栽培 (cultivated in Taiwan). Also **northern Vietnam**.
- **Wood:** pale reddish-brown, straight-grained, fine, light and soft, easy to work, warps little, cracks little on drying, not insect-attacked. Used for building, shipbuilding, furniture and joinery, and for plywood; leaves and bark are used medicinally.

## Habit

- **Mature height: to 40 m. DBH over 1 m.**
- **Silhouette, one sentence:** a tall, very straight-boled tree with an **umbrella-shaped crown** (树冠伞形) of widely spreading branches — FRPS names this feature explicitly, and it is the silhouette to build.
- **Twigs grey to grey-brown.**
- **Crown change with age:** *Estimate:* conical and narrow when young, then the leader lifts and the crown flattens into the stated umbrella form with widely spaced, long-petioled leaves.

## Branching

- **Explicitly named in FRPS: 树干挺直，树冠伞形** — the trunk is straight and the crown is umbrella-shaped. This is the single most quotable habit statement in the entry.
- **Phyllotaxy:** *Estimate:* alternate and spirally arranged, but the leaves are **clustered near the ends of the shoots** because the petioles are very long — the classic tulip-tree shoot plan.
- **First-order branch angle:** *Estimate:* 50–80°, the limbs spreading and arching outward to make the umbrella.
- **Lower branches:** *Estimate:* shed from the base with age, giving the clean straight bole that FRPS also praises (树干挺直).
- **Crown layered?** *Estimate:* broadly domed rather than layered, but with a distinct set of radiating main limbs.

## Trunk

- **Form: very straight** (挺直), cylindrical, over 1 m DBH.
- **Bark:** *not described in this entry* — FRPS gives habit, leaf, flower, fruit and uses for *L. chinense*. *Estimate:* grey, smooth with lenticels when young, becoming shallowly fissured and darker grey with age, as in *L. tulipifera*.

## Foliage

- **Leaf type:** simple, alternate.
- **Leaf size: 4–12 cm long (to 18) × 3–9.5 cm wide (to 23)** — FOC; the width range is very large because the lobed, saddle-shaped blade is broad across the top.
- **The shape, in modelling terms:** the blade is described as **马褂状 — "horse-cloth" / saddle-shaped** — and its exact construction is:
  - the **base is truncate to slightly cordate**;
  - **one lateral lobe near the base on each side** (近基部每边具1侧裂片);
  - the **apex is split into two shallow lobes** (先端具2浅裂).
  So it is a broadly obovate blade with **two basal lobes and a notched, two-lobed apex** — the famous tulip-tree leaf. This is the single most important modelling fact in this note.
- **Texture:** membranous to papery (membranous to papery); FOC notes it is **glaucous on the underside** (下面苍白色).
- **Petiole: 4–8 cm, sometimes to 16 cm** — **very long for the blade size**, and the reason the leaves flutter and the canopy looks loose. Model this explicitly.
- **Evergreen:** no — deciduous.

## Reproductive

- **Flowers cup-shaped (杯状)**, with **9 tepals in three whorls**:
  - **outer 3 tepals green, sepal-like, curved and outwardly pendulous** (外轮3片绿色，萼片状，向外弯垂);
  - **inner 2 whorls (6 tepals) erect, petal-like, obovate, 3–4 cm long, green with yellow longitudinal stripes** (绿色，具黄色纵条纹).
  The green-and-yellow striped, tulip-shaped flower is the feature that gives the genus its English name.
- **Anthers 10–16 mm long; filaments 5–6 mm.**
- **Gynoecium:** **the carpel cluster protrudes above the tepals at anthesis** (花期时雌蕊群超出花被之上); carpels yellowish-green.
- **Fruit:** an **aggregate (聚合果) 7–9 cm long**, of **winged nutlets about 6 mm long**, each with an obtuse to obtuse-mucronate apex and **1–2 seeds**.
- **Season:** flowers **May**; fruit **September–October**.
- **Reproduction biology worth knowing (affects fruit-set in a scene):** it is cross-pollinated but shows a self-incompatibility-like behaviour — **the pistil matures while the flower is still in bud, so by anthesis the stigma is already withered and yellow and has lost its receptive capacity**. Unfertilised pistels keep developing but the seed is weak, so **germination is poor**. A flowering tree therefore often carries very few viable seeds, and fruit is frequently sparse.

## The five facts that let you recognise this tree from 200 m

1. **An umbrella crown on a very straight bole.** FRPS says it outright: straight trunk, umbrella-shaped crown. The crown is broad and flat-topped on a clean vertical column — nothing else in a Chinese montane forest has this profile.
2. **A very open, airy canopy with big gaps.** The leaves are small relative to the crown and sit on very long petioles, so the canopy is see-through and flickers; you can see sky through a mature tree.
3. **Sparse, large, saddle-shaped leaves.** Only a handful of leaf shapes cover the tree, and each is a broad blade with **two lobes at the base and a notched two-lobed apex** — so the canopy texture is coarse and irregular rather than fine.
4. **Pale, almost white undersides.** The abaxial surface is glaucous, so the canopy shows as a two-tone dark-green-over-pale-grey, and wind reveals flashes of silver-grey.
5. **Tulip-shaped green flowers with yellow stripes in May, and paddle-like aggregate fruits in autumn.** The flowers are green, not coloured, which is unusual and distinctive; the fruit is a pale cylindrical cluster of winged nutlets that hangs on the tree well into the season.
