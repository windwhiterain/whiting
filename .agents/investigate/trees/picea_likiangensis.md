## Site

- **Altitude: 2500–4100 m.** Source: FOC habitat field via
  `https://www.iplant.cn/ashx/getfoc.ashx?key=Picea%20likiangensis`
  → "Mountains, ravines, river basins; 2500-4100 m."
- **Provinces (FOC, verbatim):** S Qinghai, S and W Sichuan, E Xizang (Tibet), NW Yunnan; also Bhutan.
- **Belt:** the **upper subalpine / montane conifer belt**, 2500–4100 m — the highest-belt conifer of
  this set. It is the tree of the cold, moist, high-elevation forest and forms upper-catchment forest.
  *("Vegetation belt it dominates" is my characterisation; FOC gives altitude and landform, and the
  "ravines" term is the operative ecological signal — it is a ravine and moist-slope tree.)*
- **Grows with:** other subalpine conifers (*Abies*, *Picea* relatives), and at its lower limit
  broadleaved forest; in `10-site-stand.jpg` and `02-crown.jpg` it stands among dense montane conifer
  forest on a steep slope. *(Estimate/inference — FOC gives no community list; read from the
  photographs.)*

## Habit

- **Mature height / trunk diameter — FOC, exact:** "**Trees to 50 m tall; trunk to 2.5 m d.b.h.**"
  **This is the tallest of the five species in this study and the only one with a 2 m-class trunk.**
- **Silhouette in one sentence:** a very tall, slender, strictly conical spire with a narrow
  column-like crown of pendulous, blue-grey sprays, the trunk carrying scattered conspicuous
  purple-red cones.
- **Crown change with age — the requested answer:** **the crown stays pyramidal.** FOC states the crown
  as "**pyramidal**" with no qualification, and the forest photographs in `02-crown.jpg` and
  `10-site-stand.jpg` show mature trees still holding a narrow, sharply pointed pyramidal envelope.
  **There is no juvenile-conical → mature-flat-topped transition here** — that is the opposite of
  *Pinus yunnanensis*, and it is the fact that most distinguishes a spruce from a pine at 200 m.
  *Caveat:* in dense stands, self-pruning and shading narrow the lower crown further.

## Branching

- **Whorled or spiral:** **whorled**, and strongly so — the whorl is a characteristic conifer feature
  in *Picea* and is the mechanism that builds the conical envelope. FOC does not state branch
  arrangement for this species. *Flagged as an inference.*
- **First-order branch attitude:** **horizontal to slightly ascending**, the branches leaving the
  trunk nearly perpendicular and then drooping under the weight of the foliage. This "drooping tips"
  reading is very clear in `04-branch.jpg` and `02-crown.jpg`.
- **Lower branches:** **shed**, giving a clean lower trunk; in the dense stands in `10-site-stand.jpg`
  and `02-crown.jpg` the lower bole is bare for many metres. In dense forest the self-pruning is
  strong and the crown begins very high.
- **Layered or tiered:** **yes** — the whorls produce horizontal tiers, the visible mechanism of the
  conical shape. `04-branch.jpg` shows the tier structure at close range.

## Trunk

- **Straight or contorted:** **straight**, tall and slender. `03-bark.jpg` and `01-habit.jpg` show a
  plumb bole.
- **Base:** straight-tapering, no buttressing visible. *Inferred from `03-bark.jpg`.*
- **Bark colour and texture — FOC, exact:** "bark **dull gray or brown-gray, breaking into thick,
  irregular plates**." So the bark is **dull (not shiny) grey to brown-grey, and breaks into THICK,
  IRREGULAR plates** — thick plates are the character, and they are markedly chunkier than the small
  scaly plates of *Pinus yunnanensis*. `03-bark.jpg` shows this on the stem of a mature tree: grey
  bark broken into irregular plates, heavily patched with pale lichen.
- **Branchlets — FOC, exact:** "branchlets initially **pale yellow or brownish yellow**, finally
  **gray or gray-yellow**, often sparsely pubescent, rarely with glandular hairs." The young shoots
  are noticeably pale/yellowish — this is what gives the crown its yellowish cast in
  `08-flowers.jpg` and `09-cone-macro.jpg`.
- **Winter buds — FOC, exact:** "winter buds **brown, conical, ovoid, or globose, resinous**,
  scales not reflexed, or slightly opening at base of branchlets."

## Foliage

All figures from FOC via `https://www.iplant.cn/ashx/getfoc.ashx?key=Picea%20likiangensis`:

- **Type:** single needles on pegs (the *Picea* diagnostic), arranged all round the shoot but strongly
  **two-ranked/pectinate on the lower side of the branchlets** and **directed forward on the upper
  side** — FOC's exact words: "Leaves **directed forward on upper side of branchlets, spreading on
  lower side**."
- **THE DIAGNOSTIC — 4-angled on pegs vs *Abies* flat with a notched tip:** FOC gives the
  cross-section as "**± broadly rhombic in cross section or subflattened**". A broadly rhombic
  section is the **four-angled needle of *Picea***. So: **needles are four-angled (rhombic in
  section) and attached to small woody pegs** — this is *Picea*, **not** *Abies*, whose needles are
  flat with an emarginate (notched) apex and a rounded suction-cup disc. `05-leaf.jpg` shows the
  needles standing out from the twig on their pegs, radiating rather than lying in a flat plane.
- **Length × width: 0.6–1.5 cm × 1–1.5 mm.** Note the width (1–1.5 mm) is comparable to the length's
  lower end — these are chunky, stubby needles, roughly 6–15 mm long.
- **Stomata:** 4–7 stomatal lines along each surface **adaxially**, 0–4 along each surface **abaxially**
  — the heavy adaxial banding is why the foliage reads pale/glaucous.
- **Terminal rosettes:** no — needles are singly attached along the twig.
- **Margin:** entire (needles are not toothed).
- **Evergreen:** yes. Needle persistence **4–6 years** — *ESTIMATE; FOC states no retention period.*
  The dense, dark inner crown in `03-bark.jpg` is consistent with several years' accumulation.
- **Colour:** blue-green to glaucous grey-green, with a distinctly **pale, almost silvery cast** on
  the upper surfaces and paler young growth at the shoot tips. `04-branch.jpg` and
  `10-site-stand.jpg` both show this.

## Reproductive

All from FOC via the URL above:

- **Seed cones: 4–12 cm long × 1.7–3.5 cm wide**, **ovoid-oblong or ovoid-cylindric** — i.e. the
  longest and most cylindric cones of the two spruce/fir species in this study.
- **Colour, which is the single most striking field character:** "**reddish brown or purple**,
  maturing brown, reddish brown, purplish brown, or **dark purple**." The photographs show this
  exactly — `01-habit.jpg` shows a mature tree **densely studded with purple-red cones**, and
  `07-cone-hanging.jpg` / `09-cone-macro.jpg` show the deep purple-red colour.
- **Seed scales (middle of cone):** rhombic-ovate, **1.5–2.6 × 1–1.7 cm**, base cuneate, margin
  **denticulate or undulate**, apical part narrowed into a triangular or obtusely triangular point.
  The denticulate scale margins give the ripe cone its rough, shaggy surface — clearly visible in
  `09-cone-macro.jpg`.
- **Seeds:** grey-brown, subovoid, **0.7–1.4 cm including wing**; wing pale brown, lustrous, usually
  with purplish spots, obovate-oblong.
- **Orientation and position:** the cones are **PENDANT** — hanging vertically from the ends of the
  shoots, which is the *Picea* arrangement and is the opposite of *Abies*, whose cones stand erect
  upright on the crown. See `06-cone.jpg` and `07-cone-hanging.jpg`.
- **Season:** pollination **Apr–May**; seed maturity **Sep–Oct** of the same year.

## The five facts that identify this tree from 200 m, as a silhouette

1. **A narrow, sharply pointed, strictly pyramidal spire** — a clean geometric cone, retaining that
   shape at full height and full maturity. Nothing about it is flat-topped.
2. **Great height relative to width** — up to 50 m on a 2.5 m trunk, so the crown is a slim vertical
   wedge, not a broad mass.
3. **A long clean bole with the crown starting high**, especially in dense stands where self-pruning
   is aggressive.
4. **The whole tree speckled with purple-red cones** that hang visibly beneath the foliage — at
   200 m a laden crown reads as a pale reddish stipple over blue-grey. The species is conspicuous
   from a great distance when in cone.
5. **A cool, blue-glaucous, silver-grey colour** — distinctly colder in hue than the yellow-green of
   the two pines and than the dark blue-green of *Abies delavayi*.
