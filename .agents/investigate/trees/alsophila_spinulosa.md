### Is it a relict / endemic, and what protects it

- **A relict — emphatically.** 桫椤 is described as "**中生代侏罗纪孑遗植物**", a Mesozoic Jurassic relict, and "**现存最古老的陆生维管植物之一**", one of the oldest surviving terrestrial vascular plants, and "**唯一现存木本蕨类**", the only extant woody fern. Called "**蕨类植物之王**", king of the ferns. Source: 广西植物研究所's 农素芸, quoted in [国家林业和草原局政府网](https://www.forestry.gov.cn/c/www/dzwzbh/655148.jhtml).
- **Not endemic to China** — the range runs Bangladesh to S Japan and Sri Lanka to N Thailand ([efloras](http://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=2&taxon_id=200003191)). Model it as a pan-tropical-temperate Asian relict, not a Chinese endemic.
- **Protection:** it **is listed in 《国家重点保护野生植物名录》** (the National Key Protected Wild Plants List) **and in the appendices of CITES** ([国家林业和草原局政府网](https://www.forestry.gov.cn/c/www/dzwzbh/655148.jhtml)). The reachable source says "附录" without naming which appendix, so **which CITES appendix is not recorded here** — do not assume II.
- **Ecological indicator:** the same source records that 桫椤 is a strict-environment species whose stands signal clean air, water and soil and a pristine ecosystem — useful for placing it in a scene only under closed canopy beside water.
- **Population example:** the Guangxi Leye (乐业县) reserve holds **1,243 plants in 412.2 mu** of valley, with **tallest stems 5.7 m**, protected as a dedicated 桫椤保护小区 since the 1999 discovery ([国家林业和草原局政府网](https://www.forestry.gov.cn/c/www/dzwzbh/655148.jhtml)).

## Habit

- **Trunk height 5–15 m, sometimes more than 20 m.** Flora of China: "Trunk 5-15 m or sometimes more than 20 m". The Guangxi Leye stand's tallest stems measured **5.7 m** ([国家林业和草原局政府网](https://www.forestry.gov.cn/c/www/dzwzbh/655148.jhtml)).
- **Trunk diameter 10–20 cm, or more** — [efloras FOC 2-3](http://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=2&taxon_id=200003191).
- **Silhouette, in one sentence:** a single straight unbranched vertical column, shaggy with roots and old leaf bases and *widest at the ground*, carrying at its very top one radial crown of 10–16 long feather-shaped fronds that arch outward and then droop — a slender parasol on a pole, like a small palm or a badminton shuttlecock.
- **How the crown changes with age:** a young plant is a single column of tightly crowded fronds, nearly spherical and small ([06-crown-radial-top-view.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/06-crown-radial-top-view.jpg) shows a low juvenile crown). Each year the crown adds a whorl at the apex and the column lengthens by one internode; old fronds die but their stipes stay on the column as a permanent skirt, so an old plant has a **bare lower column, a live crown, and an irregular brown skirt of dead stipes just below the crown** ([13-stipe-skirt-dead-fronds.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/13-stipe-skirt-dead-fronds.jpg)). There is no second flush along the trunk — that is the point of the persistent bases.

## Branching

**There is no branching. None at all above ground.** This species has no woody branch system whatsoever, and any procedural tree system driven by a branch graph cannot represent it. Everything below is the crown, which is a single radial array on one axis.

- **How the crown of fronds is arranged:** a single **radial crown of fronds at the apex of the trunk**, each frond unbranched, the whole set leaving the trunk top in all directions like the ribs of an umbrella. There is **no secondary or tertiary order** — a frond is stipe + lamina, and the lamina is composed of pinnae directly on the rachis.
- **Number of fronds in a crown: 10–16.** **ESTIMATE, counted** from [06-crown-radial-top-view.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/06-crown-radial-top-view.jpg) (13–15 radiating fronds clearly separable from directly above) and [02-crown-from-below.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/02-crown-from-below.jpg) (11–12 stipes countable at the trunk apex). Flora of China gives no count.
- **Angle at which the fronds leave the trunk: the stipes leave at roughly 45–70° from the trunk axis**, rising out of the crown centre and then arching over so that the distal half of every frond hangs below horizontal. Read from [02-crown-from-below.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/02-crown-from-below.jpg) and [04-crown-apex-stipes.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/04-crown-apex-stipes.jpg), **marked as read from photographs.** FOC does not give a frond-departure angle.
- **Crown diameter vs. trunk height:** from [11-whole-plant-radial-crown.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/11-whole-plant-radial-crown.jpg) and [01-habit-whole-tree-wild.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/01-habit-whole-tree-wild.jpg), the crown spreads to roughly **1.0–1.5 × the trunk height**. On a 7 m column the crown is 7–10 m across. **ESTIMATE.**
- **Is the crown layered?** No, and it must not be: the crown is one flat-topped radial rosette. New fronds rise from the centre, old ones hang from the rim, which produces a shallow dome of strongly arched fronds — not tiers.

## Trunk

The "trunk" is **not wood**. It is a mechanical column of **persistent leaf bases and adventitious roots**, and it must be modelled as such.

- **Flora of China, verbatim:** "Trunk 5-15 m or sometimes more than 20 m, 10-20 cm in diam. or more, **densely covered by adventitious roots**." ([efloras FOC 2-3](http://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=2&taxon_id=200003191))
- **Straight, not contorted.** Every photograph shows a plumb, essentially cylindrical column with no taper toward the top and no lean. It gets *wider* toward the ground because the adventitious-root mantle flares out ([01-habit-whole-tree-wild.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/01-habit-whole-tree-wild.jpg)).
- **The surface, from FOC:**
  - "**Fronds 2-pinnate-pinnatifid; stipes persistent and forming an irregular skirt**, lustrous, greenish, **purple-black toward base**, 30-50 cm, with **spines** and lustrous brown scales"
  - "**scales on trunks and stipes dark brown, lustrous, several cells thick, stiff, narrowly lanceolate, apex dark brown, setose, with pale and thin toothed margins, scales falling off leaving sharp prickles at base**"
- **In words for a modeller:** the column surface is **dark brown to blackish-purple, matte-lustrous, densely shaggy**, made of (a) a mantle of fine adventitious roots, especially heavy in the lower half; (b) tightly packed old stipe bases; and (c) **dark brown, stiff, lanceolate, several-cell-thick scales with a bristly setose tip and a pale fringed margin**. Those scales are **detachable, and where they have fallen off the column is left with sharp conical prickles.** So the trunk texture is: shaggy at the base (roots), finely prickled over the middle (shed scales), and clothed in the upper third by the persistent stipe skirt.
- **Photographic confirmation:** [03-trunk-persistent-leaf-bases.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/03-trunk-persistent-leaf-bases.jpg) is the trunk reference — it shows the packed persistent bases, the dark scales, and the pale prickles left where scales have dropped. [04-crown-apex-stipes.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/04-crown-apex-stipes.jpg) shows the same surface where the live stipes leave the apex, including the brown scale-clothed and spiny lower stipes. [13-stipe-skirt-dead-fronds.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/13-stipe-skirt-dead-fronds.jpg) shows the dead-frond skirt hanging down the upper trunk.
- **Diameter: 10–20 cm, or more** — flora. With the adventitious-root mantle, the *apparent* basal diameter in [01-habit-whole-tree-wild.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/01-habit-whole-tree-wild.jpg) is noticeably larger than the bare column.

## Reproductive

- **Sori position: at the forks of the lateral veinlets.** Flora of China: "**Sori at forks of lateral veinlets**" ([efloras](http://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=2&taxon_id=200003191)). That places them **not** in a round marginal sorus but **along the lateral veins of each pinnule segment, one sorus at each fork, in two flanking rows down each side of the segment midrib.** The photographs show exactly this regular paired arrangement along the segment midribs.
- **Indusium: yes, and it is a ball.** "**Indusia globose, thin, membranous, completely enveloping sori from base when young, irregularly falling off at maturity.**" Three stages are photographed:
  - **immature**, the indusium a smooth translucent green globe completely hiding the sporangia — [07-sori-indusia-immature-green.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/07-sori-indusia-immature-green.jpg), closer in [14-sori-indusia-close.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/14-sori-indusia-close.jpg);
  - **mature**, the indusium brown and crumpled, hanging partly off the sorus so the spore mass shows — [08-sori-indusia-mature-fallen.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/08-sori-indusia-mature-fallen.jpg).
- **Indusium diameter** is not given by FOC. **ESTIMATE 1.5–2.5 mm**, from the ratio of the globose indusium to a pinnule segment given as "up to 7 × 4 mm" in [07-sori-indusia-immature-green.jpg](C:/resource/whiting/out/refs/alsophila_spinulosa/07-sori-indusia-immature-green.jpg). The balls are about a third of the segment's length across.
- **Sori per pinnule segment:** 1 sorus at each lateral-veinlet fork, i.e. **1 per fork, with one fork per side per segment**, so roughly **2 sori per segment**, in two flanking rows. Read from the photographs; FOC gives only the rule.
- No flowers, no fruit, no seed — this is a fern and reproduces by spores only.

## The five facts that let you recognise this tree from 200 m away, as a silhouette

1. **A pole with a parasol.** A single straight, vertical, unbranched column carrying **all** of its foliage in one crown at the very top. Nothing grows off the column. A model with a single flush at the crown and a clean bare column below is right; anything with side branches or a tapered trunk is wrong.
2. **The column is widest at the ground.** It is a shaggy cone of adventitious roots and old leaf bases that flares into the substrate, and it does **not** taper upward like a woody trunk. Read the silhouette bottom-heavy.
3. **The crown is a shallow dome of strongly arched fronds**, wider than deep, every frond leaving the apex at 45–70° and arching down so its tip hangs below horizontal. From 200 m the crown outline is a shallow bowl, not a sphere.
4. **The crown fringe is combed into separate flat feather vanes.** Each frond is a flat ladder of pinnae and the vanes are visibly individual at 200 m, with gaps of sky between them. A model that fills the crown with a solid mass loses this entirely.
5. **A brown fringe of dead fronds hangs just below the live crown**, marking the skirt of persistent stipe bases in the upper third of the column. That dark collar under the green crown is a reliable recognition cue at range.
