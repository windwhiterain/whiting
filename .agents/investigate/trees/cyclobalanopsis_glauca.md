# 青冈 — Cyclobalanopsis glauca

*Measured 2026-10-18. One question: what does this tree look like, and how does it grow?*

> **Naming note.** GBIF's backbone **sinks *Cyclobalanopsis* into *Quercus*** and returns *Cyclobalanopsis glauca* (Thunb.) Oerst. as a **HOMOTYPIC_SYNONYM of *Quercus glauca*** (key 2881587, family Fagaceae). 中国植物志 and Flora of China keep *Cyclobalanopsis* as a separate genus. The plant is the same either way; only the genus placement differs.

## Site

- **Altitude: 60–2600 m** — an exceptionally wide vertical range, from near sea level to high mountains.
- **Habitat:** mountain slopes and valleys (山坡或沟谷).
- **Vegetation belt:** a **component of evergreen broadleaf forest**, and of **mixed evergreen-and-deciduous broadleaf forest** — the defining structural tree of the subtropical evergreen forest.
- **Grows with:** the other evergreen broadleaves; as a minor component it also enters deciduous mixtures.

## Habit

- **Mature height: to 20 m. DBH to 1 m.** Evergreen tree (常绿乔木).
- **Silhouette, one sentence:** a medium-tall evergreen oak with a dense, rounded, dark, glossy-leaved crown of thick spreading limbs — the archetype of the subtropical evergreen broadleaf canopy tree.
- **Young branchlets glabrous (小枝无毛).**
- **Crown change with age:** *Estimate:* conical when young, becoming broad and rounded with age; the crown stays dense because the foliage is evergreen and leathery.

## Branching

- **Not stated** in FRPS beyond the trunk and leaf characters.
- **Phyllotaxy:** **leaves alternate** (叶互生 in the related genus account; for this species the entry does not say) — *estimate:* alternate, spirally arranged.
- **First-order branch angle:** *Estimate:* 40–70°, the limbs thick and ascending to spreading, the crown rounded.
- **Lower branches:** *Estimate:* persist on a 20 m evergreen; evergreen oaks of this type keep a full crown fairly low.
- **Crown layered?** *Estimate:* loosely layered, the thick limbs forking repeatedly into a dense outer shell.

## Trunk

- **Form:** straight, cylindrical.
- **Bark:** *not described in this entry* — the FRPS account gives the leaf, flower and fruit in detail and omits the bark. *Estimate:* grey, smooth on young stems, becoming shallowly fissured and dark greyish-brown on old trunks, as is typical of the group.
- **Not spiny.**

## Foliage

- **Leaf type:** simple, **coriaceous (革质)** — stiff and leathery.
- **Leaf size: 6–13 cm long × 2–5.5 cm wide.**
- **Shape: obovate-elliptic or long-elliptic** (倒卵状椭圆形或长椭圆形).
- **Apex: acuminate or shortly caudate** (渐尖或短尾状). **Base: rounded or broadly cuneate** (圆形或宽楔形).
- **Margin: sparsely serrate only on the upper half of the blade; entire below** (叶缘中部以上有疏锯齿). This half-serrate margin is a good identification character.
- **Venation: 9–13 lateral veins per side**; the **secondary veins on the underside are prominent** (叶背支脉明显).
- **Petiole: 1–3 cm.**
- **Indumentum:** upper surface glabrous; **lower surface with neat, appressed, white uniseriate hairs (整齐平伏白色单毛)**, falling away with age, and **often carrying white scales (白色鳞秕)**.
- **Evergreen:** yes — the defining habit character.
- **Colour:** *not stated in the entry.* *Estimate:* glossy dark green above, paler and greyer or faintly glaucous beneath.

## Reproductive

- **Male inflorescences (catkins): 5–6 cm long**, the axis covered in greyish tomentum.
- **Infructescence stalk: 1.5–3 cm**, bearing **2–3 fruits**.
- **Cupule (壳斗): bowl-shaped, enclosing only the **lower 1/3 to 1/2** of the nut** (包着坚果1/3-1/2), **0.9–1.4 cm across × 0.6–0.8 cm high**, covered with short hairs.
- **Cupule scales: fused into 5–6 concentric rings (小苞片合生成5-6条同心环带), each ring entire or finely notched, set closely together.** Those concentric rings are the classic *Cyclobalanopsis* cupule and are the best diagnostic detail on the tree.
- **Nut: ovoid, long-ovoid or ellipsoid, 0.9–1.4 cm across × 1–1.6 cm high**, glabrous or short-hairy; **the basal scar is flat or slightly convex**.
- **Season:** flowers **April–May**; fruit **October**.
- **Economy:** seeds are **60–70% starch** (fodder, brewing); bark holds **16% tannin**, cupule **10–15%**.

## The five facts that let you recognise this tree from 200 m

1. **A dense, rounded, dark, glossy evergreen crown.** It holds full leaf through the winter while its neighbours are bare, which is the single most obvious thing about it in any Chinese subtropical forest.
2. **Heavy, thick, spreading limbs.** The tree reads as structurally stout — thick branches, a thick bole — rather than the airy look of a birch or the spire of a conifer.
3. **A smooth, even, unbroken canopy texture.** The leathery, closely set leaves produce a flat, polished crown surface with no ragged edges or gaps.
4. **Bare below, then a sudden dense shell.** The trunk is clear for a good height and the crown begins abruptly, so the outline is a clean stem-and-dome.
5. **A crop of small acorns on short stalks in autumn.** Clusters of 2–3 nuts each in a shallow scaly cup, held on 1.5–3 cm stalks — small, but a distinct pale-brown dotted texture over the crown in October.
