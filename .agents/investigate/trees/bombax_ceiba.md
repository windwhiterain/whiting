*Measured 2026-10-18. One question: what does this tree look like, and how does it grow?*

## Site

- **Altitude:** below **1400 m**, stated as 海拔1400(-1700)米以下 — locally to about 1700 m. (FRPS.)
- **Habitat:** 干热河谷 (dry-hot river valleys) and 稀树草原 (open dry woodland). It also occurs in gully monsoon forest (沟谷季雨林). Planted widely as a street tree.
- **Provinces:** Yunnan, Sichuan, Guizhou, Guangxi, Jiangxi, Guangdong, Fujian, Taiwan.
- **Vegetation belt:** subtropical **dry-hot valley / dry savanna**, not montane forest. This is the defining site fact: the tree is a deciduous giant of seasonally dry, high-heat country.
- **Grows with:** sparse drought-adapted woodland; in China the northern limit is the hot dry valley scrub around the Jinsha and Yuan rivers.

## Habit

- **Mature height:** to **25 m** (FRPS 高可达25米; FOC "Trees to 25 m tall").
- **Trunk diameter:** not stated in either flora text. See the modelling table — estimated.
- **Silhouette, one sentence:** a very tall clean bole carrying a broad, flat-topped, spreading crown of near-horizontal limbs — a parasol or mushroom outline whose crown is wider than the crown is deep.
- **Crown change with age:** a young tree is a narrow spiky cone with a continuous central leader; as it matures the leader is lost, the crown lifts and flattens, and the lower branches that carried the cone are shed, leaving a long bare bole under a wide plate of foliage. *The young-to-mature transition is my reading of the two statements "young trees are very spiny" and "branches spreading", not a stated description.*

## Branching

- **Phyllotaxy:** not stated by FRPS or FOC. **Estimate:** first-order limbs arise in pseudo-whorls of 3–5 near the top of the bole and spiral lower down.
- **First-order branch angle:** **near-horizontal, spreading.** FRPS 分枝平展 ("branches spread flat"); FOC "branches spreading". *Estimate from those words and from reference photo `03`: limbs leave the trunk at roughly 60–80° from vertical, flattening toward the rim.*
- **Lower branches:** *Estimate:* shed as the crown rises, so a mature tree shows a clean bole over its lower 40–55%.
- **Crown layered?** *Estimate:* yes — a shallow upper plate over a lower, sparser, more horizontal tier, rather than a single solid shell.

## Trunk

- **Form:** straight, cylindrical, erect; **buttressed at the base** (FOC "trunk buttressed").
- **Armed:** **stout conical spines on the trunk of young trees** — FRPS 幼树的树干通常有圆锥状的粗刺; FOC "usually very spiny on young trees". The spines are shed with age; an old tree's bole is smooth.
- **Bark:** **gray-white** (树皮灰白色). Smooth on young stems; greying and lightly fissured on old ones. *Texture with age is an estimate — the flora gives colour only.*

## Foliage

- **Leaf type:** **palmately compound (掌状复叶), 5–7 leaflets.**
- **Leaflet size:** **10–16 cm long × 3.5–5.5 cm wide** (FRPS and FOC agree).
- **Leaflet shape:** oblong to oblong-lanceolate (长圆形至长圆状披针形); apex **acuminate** (渐尖); base **broad or tapering** (阔或渐狭); margin **entire** (全缘).
- **Venation:** 15–17 pairs of lateral veins, ascending, with one finer secondary vein between each pair; very fine reticulation raised on both surfaces.
- **Petiole:** **10–20 cm** (叶柄长10-20厘米). Petiolules 1.5–4 cm. Stipules minute.
- **Indumentum:** glabrous on both surfaces (两面均无毛).
- **Deciduous:** yes — 落叶大乔木.
- **Colour:** the Chinese and English flora texts give no colour. *Estimate:* mid-green above, slightly paler and matte beneath, reddening before leaf-fall.
- **Distribution along a shoot:** all 5–7 leaflets radiate from the tip of one long petiole, in one whorl, like fingers of an open hand — the leaves do not alternate along a twig, they cluster at the end of a long stalk.

## Reproductive

- **Flowers:** solitary, terminal or in the leaf axils of branch tips (花单生枝顶叶腋); **ca. 10 cm in diameter**.
- **Colour:** usually **red**, sometimes orange-red (通常红色，有时橙红色).
- **Calyx:** cup-shaped, 2–3 cm long, 3–5 lobes, each semi-orbicular ca. 1.5 × 2.3 cm, outside glabrous, inside densely yellowish-sericeous.
- **Petals:** obovate-oblong, **8–10 × 3–4 cm**, thick and fleshy, stellate-puberulent both faces.
- **Stamens:** outer series in **5 fascicles** of more than 10 each (the long ones); inner series bifid; a central ring of 10 shorter, entire filaments.
- **Fruit:** a **capsule**, ellipsoid and blunt-ended, **10–15 cm long × 4.5–5 cm across**, densely grey-white villous and stellate-puberulent, full of the cottony floss the kapok is named for.
- **Seeds:** many, obovate, smooth.
- **Season:** flowers **March–April**; fruit ripens in summer.
- **Phenology that matters most:** in dry-hot country **the flowers open before the leaves** (在干热地区，花先叶开放). Only in monsoon-forest climate do flower and leaf appear together.

## The five facts that let you recognise this tree from 200 m

1. **A parasol.** An immense, flat-topped crown on near-horizontal limbs — wider than it is deep, with a flat or slightly domed top, not a cone and not a ball. This silhouette alone separates it from almost every other tropical tree.
2. **A long clean bole.** The crown starts high; a mature tree has no branches over much of its trunk, so the outline is a narrow column widening abruptly into a plate.
3. **A spiky grey column in youth.** The bole of a young tree is bristling with stout conical spines — unmistakable at close range and the single most diagnostic surface detail.
4. **Plank buttresses at the foot.** The base flares into vertical boards (板状根) rather than a simple round collar.
5. **Red flowers on a bare tree.** In the dry season the whole crown reads as scattered scarlet points on grey wood, with no leaves at all. In leaf the crown is a **loose, pale, open** mass — the palmate leaves are widely spaced, so light passes through — not the dense dark blob of a rainforest tree.
