# The raster mapping is measured, not assumed

Measured 2026-10-13, Cycles 5.3.0 at `C:\resource\cycles\install\cycles.exe`.

## The question

To say where a piece of geometry lands in a traced frame you need four conventions, and every one
of them is read from source or assumed:

- is `fov` the **full** angle or the half angle;
- does raster `x` increase with the camera's **local `+X`**;
- does raster `y` increase **downwards**, and so is the camera's local `+Y` the **top** of the
  picture;
- is the mapping **isotropic**, or does the sensor fit stretch one axis.

Everything downstream of a projection depends on all four, and a wrong answer in any of them is
invisible: the picture still looks like a picture. `probe-camera-ray.py` answers a different
question — it compares the **forward** direction of the two backends — and a projection error
leaves the forward exactly where it was.

## The method

A hand-written document, `out/probe-camera-calibration.cycles.xml`, **not generated by whiting
and derived from no scene**. The camera has **no rotation and no scale**, so it looks down its own
`+Z` with `+Y` up, and Cycles being z-up that is straight up out of the ground. Four emissive
markers, each a 0.3 x 0.3 quad at a depth of 5, stand where the projection says they belong:
`(0, 0, 5)`, `(2.569, 0, 5)`, `(0, 2.569, 5)` and `(0, -2.569, 5)`. `tan(0.5 * 0.95) = 0.51381`,
so `2.569` is exactly the half frame at that depth.

Emission rather than a lit surface, because **an emitter draws wherever the camera draws it** and
nothing about lighting can move it. `emission_color` alone is not enough —
`node_principled_bsdf.osl:193` is `if (EmissionStrength != 0.0 && EmissionColor != color(0.0))`
and `EmissionStrength` **defaults to zero**, so a document carrying only a colour reads back
exactly as written and renders byte for byte the baseline.

Rendered at 32 samples on CPU, `--width 1024 --height 1024`, and the drawn regions read off the
pixels by `out/probe-regions.py`.

## What it says

| marker, world | what it tests | measured centroid | expected |
|---|---|---|---|
| `(0, 0, 5)` | the centre | `(0.4995, 0.4995)` | `(0.5, 0.5)` |
| `(2.569, 0, 5)` | local `+X`, the frame's right edge | `(0.9844, 0.4995)` | `x = 1.0` |
| `(0, 2.569, 5)` | local `+Y` | `(0.4995, 0.9844)` | an edge |
| `(0, -2.569, 5)` | local `-Y` | `(0.4995, 0.0146)` | the other edge |

**`fov` is the full angle.** The two side markers sit at 0.9844 and 0.0146 rather than at 1.0 and
0.0 because their regions are clipped by the frame edge: the uncut centre marker is 0.0576 wide
against the 0.0584 the projection asks for, **one and a half percent**, and that is the whole of
the error. Read as a half angle they would have landed at 0.75 and 0.25.

**Raster `y` runs downwards and the camera's local `+Y` is the top of the picture.** The marker on
local `+Y` came out at the **bottom** (`y = 0.9844`) and the one on `-Y` at the **top**
(`y = 0.0146`). This is the one convention that was a pure assumption before, and it was the
plausible-but-unverified kind.

**Raster `x` runs with the camera's local `+X`**, and the mapping is **isotropic**: the uncut
marker is 0.0576 in x and 0.0576 in y.

So for a square frame at `fov = 0.95`:

```text
raster_x = 0.5 + (local_x / local_z) / (2 tan(0.475))
raster_y = 0.5 - (local_y / local_z) / (2 tan(0.475))
```

with the camera's own axes reached from the world as `R * S`: the loader applies
`tfm = tfm * scale` (`src/app/cycles_xml.cpp:706-710`), so the scale lands on the **local**
frame, on the right, and negates the local `X` column only. `S * R` — a world-side scale —
is not what the loader computes, and a document written for it renders a scene the camera
is pointed away from. That was measured on 2026-10-13 at `afb0c0b`; see
[do-the-backends-read-one-environment-the-same-way.md](do-the-backends-read-one-environment-the-same-way.md).

**This file first said `S * R`, in an earlier version of this paragraph, and it was wrong in the
same way the shipped correction was.** It is recorded here because the error is not visible in
the four markers below, all of which a world-side scale would also have placed correctly on a
camera with no rotation.

## What this makes checkable

A projected world position can now be compared with a measured one to about one percent, which is
what
[every-sky-agrees-and-the-ground-does-not.md](every-sky-agrees-and-the-ground-does-not.md) needed
and did not have. **Projecting `sunlight`'s floor with these conventions says the floor fills the
lower half of the frame**, and the traced frame has it in 10.37% of it.
