# Chinese vernacular timber frame and roof: what rules must a procedural building generator express?

**Question.** For south-west Chinese traditional vernacular architecture — Chongqing 吊脚楼 (diaojiaolou,
"stilt houses"), Yunnan and Guizhou ethnic-minority dwellings, and 古城/古镇 settlement fabric — what
are the **structural and geometric rules** of the timber frame and the roof, with numbers, so that a
procedural generator can model from them? 官式 buildings (palaces, temples built to 《营造法式》 or
《工程做法则例》) are surveyed **only as a measuring standard and a contrast**, never as a default.

**Date.** 2026-10-18. Access date for every URL below is 2026-10-18.

**Status / honesty rule for this note.** Every number below carries a source tag. Where a dimension
could not be found in a [heritage survey] or [academic] source it is written **not established**,
together with the measurement that would settle it. Numbers produced by arithmetic on a cited primary
rule are tagged **[derived]** — they are the rule's own arithmetic, not a measurement. **Nothing here is
a guess.**

**Read this before using the Numbers section.** The three primary sources that were read in full are
the **official** ones — 《营造法式》, 梁思成's annotated edition, and 《工程做法》. **Almost every number
in this note is an official number and is therefore a contrast, not a vernacular default.** Only two
vernacular sources were obtained, both from one region, one of them a single 1916 building complex.
That imbalance is the honest state of the research and is stated again at the head of every affected
table rather than buried here.

House style follows [vegetation-modeling-techniques.md](vegetation-modeling-techniques.md).

## Verdict

A Chinese timber building is not a mass with openings; it is a **skeleton of posts and beams that
carries the roof, with walls hung on it**. The load path is 瓦 → 望板/椽 → 檩 (purlins) → 瓜柱 (short
posts) and 梁 (beams) → 柱 (columns) → 柱础 (stone plinth) → ground. Three of the current frame's
assumptions break at once. **A roof is not a plate**: it is a doubly-curved surface generated by a
step sequence of purlin elevations, with a ridge line, two eave lines, an overhang measured from the
eave purlin and not from the wall face, and a tile covering whose own thickness and corrugation are
part of the section. **A wall need not be the structure**: in a 穿斗式 (chuandou) frame the posts are
close-set and the walls are infill between posts, so the plan outline and the load path are different
polygons. **The floor need not sit on the ground**: a 干栏式 (ganlan) frame stands on stilts, so the
ground plane and the first-floor plane are independent, and the underside of the building is an
exterior elevation, not a hidden face. The generator must therefore be able to express **a frame as a
graph of members with named joints**, **a roof as a surface swept between an ordered set of purlin
stations**, and **a building on a stilts with two independent elevations**. What the flat-plate
vocabulary cannot express is the roof profile — and that is precisely the part this note treats most
carefully, because 举折 (Song), 举架 (Qing) and 提栈 (Jiangnan) are *three different algorithms* for
generating a curve, not three different pitch numbers.

### What the Qing official text says about each (contrast, not default)

《工程做法则例》 **卷四 · 玖檁楼房大木图式** [S2] gives a complete worked example of a nine-purlin
two-storey building, and it is worth reading as a *specification of relationships* even where the
target is vernacular:

- 「凡下檐柱以面闊拾分之捌定高低，拾分之柒定徑寸。如面闊壹丈叄尺，得柱高壹丈肆寸，徑玖寸壹分。」
  — eave-column **height = 0.8 × 面阔**, **diameter = 0.07 × 面阔**. At 面阔 13尺 the text computes
  10尺4寸 and 9寸1分 [S2]. **[derived]** Column slenderness 径:高 = 1 : 11.4.
- 「凡檁木以面闊定長短…徑寸俱與下檐柱同。」 — **purlin diameter = eave-column diameter**, purlin
  length = 面阔 [S2].
- 「以檩徑拾分之叄定徑寸，如檩徑玖寸壹分，得徑貳寸柒分」 — **rafter diameter = 0.3 × purlin
  diameter**; 9.1寸 × 0.3 = 2.73寸 = 2寸7分 [S2].
- 「每椽空檔隨椽徑壹分」 — **rafter gap = one rafter diameter**, i.e. centre-to-centre = 2 × 椽径 [S2].
  Note this is a *gap*, whereas 营造法式 gives a *centre-to-centre* 广 — the two are not the same
  dimension and are not interchangeable.
- 「照檐柱高拾分之叄」 — **eave overhang = 0.3 × column height**; 10.4尺 × 0.3 = 3尺1寸2分 [S2].
- 「如出廊深肆尺，又加出檐尺寸…共長柒尺壹寸貳分。又按壹壹伍加舉，得通長捌尺壹寸捌分。」
  — eave rafter length = (verandah depth + overhang) × **1.15**; (4 + 3.12) × 1.15 = 8.19尺 [S2] **[derived]**.
- 「如步架深肆尺，按陸舉加之，得高貳尺肆寸」 (金瓜柱) and 「按捌舉加之，得高叄尺貳寸」 (脊瓜柱) —
  the 举架 coefficients for a 4尺 step are **六举 for the 金 step and 八举 for the 脊 step** [S2];
  4 × 0.6 = 2.4尺 and 4 × 0.8 = 3.2尺 [derived].
- 「如出廊肆尺，按伍舉加之，得高貳尺」 (棋枋板) — the lowest roof step of this frame is **五举** [S2];
  4 × 0.5 = 2尺 [derived].
- 「凡上檐金柱…如步架深肆尺，按肆舉加之，得高壹尺陸寸」 — a *column* height from the step at
  **四举** [S2]; do not confuse this with a roof step.

**Roof tile sizes, official** [S2] — quoted because the *shape* of the schedule (tile size drives the
瓦口) transfers even when the sizes do not:

| tile | 中高 (centre height) | 瓦口净高 | 瓦口厚 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 头号板瓦 | 二寸三分 | 四寸 | 一寸 |
| 貳号板瓦 | 一寸七寸三分 | 三寸四分 | 八分 |
| 叄号板瓦 | 一寸五寸三分 | 三寸 | 七分 |

「其厚俱按瓦口凈高尺寸肆分之壹」 — 瓦口 thickness = ¼ of its net height [S2] **[derived]** checks out
on all three rows. 「如用筒瓦，卽隨頭、貳、叄號板瓦之瓦口，應除山子壹分之高厚」 — a 筒瓦 (barrel /
concave tile) 瓦口 is the same as the 板瓦 瓦口 **minus the 山子 (1分)** in both height and thickness
[S2].

## The structural systems

Four systems are named in the survey literature; each is a **different way of getting the purlins up
off the ground**, and the difference is one of *load path*, not of appearance.

### 1. 穿斗式 chuāndǒu shì — "chuan-dou", the tied-post / column-and-tie system

- **Member set:** 柱 (close-set), 穿枋 (passing through the post heads), 檩 resting **directly on the
  posts**, 瓜柱 only where a purlin must step up between purlin rows, 椽, 望板, 瓦, and infill 墙.
- **Load path:** tile → 椽 → 檩 → **the post head itself** → 柱础 → ground. A beam never spans a bay in
  the vertical sense; the posts go straight to the plinth.
- **Bay logic (the decisive rule):** **column spacing equals purlin spacing (柱距 = 檩距), and every
  column carries a purlin.** Each bay is one purlin bay. A row of posts is a row of purlin props.
- **Consequence for a generator:** the plan is a dense column grid; the roof plan is the same grid
  with the ridge deleted. A "bay" in plan and a "步架" (bùjià, one purlin step) in section are **the
  same number**, so section depth = (number of bays − 1) × column spacing + overhangs.
- **Where it dominates:** the south-west. It is the standard frame for Han-line and Miao/Yao/Dong
  vernacular houses in Hunan–Guizhou–Chongqing–Sichuan–Yunnan–Guangxi, precisely because it needs few
  long timbers, only small-diameter logs, and it can be raised by a carpenter team working without a
  crane. *(Dominance claim: see §Sources and §Not established — the regional distribution is asserted in
  the survey literature and in the heritage descriptions, but no tabulated survey with building counts
  per system was obtained this session.)*

**The one surveyed Chongqing example, and what it proves.** The 重庆市文物考古研究院 publishes a
survey of 桥梁民居 (Qiaoliang dwellings), 小南海镇桥梁村, 黔江区, built 1916 — declared a 区级
文物保护单位 in 2014 — which states the construction system in one sentence [S11a]:

> 「房屋为**穿斗式梁架、悬山式屋顶，青瓦覆盖**。」

and describes the load path as [S11a]:

> 「每条檩子下方以木柱支撑，穿枋横向连接每根柱子形成排架，柱子之间则通过**斗枋**实现纵向联系。
> 这种工艺的特点是**能用较小的柱子建造较大的房屋**，屋顶的瓦、檩、椽等重量经柱子传递至地面，檩与柱
> 之间依靠榫卯结构联结，以此增强房屋的稳定性。」

This is **[heritage survey]** support for the three claims that matter to a generator, in the target
region:

1. **The 穿斗 bay logic is confirmed as an actual joinery, not a diagram**: *a post under every purlin*,
   穿枋 tying the posts across, 斗枋 tying them along. A generator's plan grid is therefore the purlin
   grid, and the posts stand **outside** the wall panels.
2. **The system is chosen for stability at height**, explicitly because the 正厅 is 2–3 storeys [S11a] —
   so the multi-storey 穿斗 case is the reason the system exists in the south-west.
3. **The roof covering is laid without an intermediate board layer in this type** — see §Numbers.

The same survey records the regional frame variety for 黔江 without selecting one default: 「以木结构
为主，兼石和小青瓦；**或抬梁式梁架，或穿斗式梁架；或歇山式顶，或悬山式顶**」 — both systems and both
roof forms occur in one district [S11a]. **A generator must not treat either as the regional default
without a site survey.**

### 2. 抬梁式 táiliáng shì — the post-and-beam system

- **Member set:** 柱 at the **edges of each bay only**, 梁 (span beams) crossing the bay, 瓜柱 on the
  beams, 檩 on the 瓜柱, 穿枋 absent or secondary, plus 隔架枋.
- **Load path:** tile → 椽 → 檩 → 瓜柱 → 梁 (spanning the bay) → 柱 at the bay edges → 柱础 → ground.
- **Bay logic:** **columns stand at the two edges of a bay; a beam spans between them.** The purlin
  rows are *independent* of the column grid — there are more purlin rows than column rows, which is
  what the 瓜柱 are for.
- **Consequence for a generator:** two separate one-dimensional grids — a column grid and a purlin
  grid — plus the rule that purlin rows not coinciding with a column row are served by a 瓜柱 standing
  on a beam. Modelling this needs a member *graph*; a grid of openings cannot express it.
- **Where it dominates:** north and north-east China (Shanxi, Shaanxi, Hebei, Shandong, Beijing) and the
  Jiangnan halls; it appears in the south-west mainly in the more substantial and more ceremonial
  dwellings — temples, 宗祠 (ancestral halls), 土司 (native-official) seats — rather than in ordinary
  houses.

### 3. 井干式 jǐnggān shì — the log-cabin system

- **Member set:** horizontal logs (原木) stacked in courses at the corners with a crossed joint, a
  single-storey wall that IS the structure, a simple gable or 悬山/硬山 roof carried on purlins or on
  the log walls, no post grid at all in the strict version.
- **Load path:** roof → wall of stacked logs → ground. There is no frame; the wall is a bearing wall.
- **Consequence for a generator:** a *single-layer* structure whose corner joint is a crossed
  interlocking of two members. The generator needs a **stacked-course** primitive with a corner-joint
  type, not a post-and-beam graph.
- **Where it dominates:** forest frontier regions — Jilin/Heilongjiang (长白山, 锦江村), the Greater and
  Lesser Khingan, and the Manchu/Evenki belt. Gao, Hama, Kobayashi, Fujikawa & Ando document the
  Jinjiang village (锦江村) houses and their plan/section transformation since 1930 [S9]. **It is not a
  south-west type**, and it is listed here because the generator that can draw it can draw the *corner
  joint* that some south-west border dwellings share, and because it is the clearest case where the
  "wall is not a plate" rule is absolute.

### 4. 干栏式 gānlán shì — the stilted / pile-supported system

- **Member set:** 柱 (or 木桩 bamboo piles) raised off the ground; horizontal floor beams (搁栅 /
  承重) carrying 楼板 (floor joists) and 楼板 (floor boards); the frame above; often an open or
  half-open undercroft of posts and braces; access by 梯 (ladder) or 楼梯.
- **Load path:** roof → frame → floor beams → **posts** → ground. The floor is a **second ground
  plane** carried on the frame; nothing about it is derived from the terrain.
- **Consequence for a generator:** `floor level` and `ground level` are independent parameters; a
  `clear height under the floor` parameter exists; the underside elevation is an authored surface,
  not a bottom face. **This is the single clearest break with the current plate vocabulary.**
- **Where it dominates:** the south-west (Yunnan Xishuangbanna Dai 竹楼, Guizhou/Zhuang, the Miao and
  Tujia of the middle Yangtze, and the 吊脚楼 of the Three Gorges and the Wu River gorges), plus
  South-East Asia. It is the *ground* form the south-west inherits: **吊脚楼 is 干栏式 applied to one
  slope of a hillside house.**

### 5. Mixed systems, and how they stack

The systems are not exclusive. Two mixtures matter to the generator:

- **抬梁 below, 穿斗 above.** The lower storey is 抬梁式 when the floor must stay flat and the posts
  must not pass through it; the upper roof structure is 穿斗式 when the roof needs many purlin rows and
  small timbers. The transition is a plane, and the two systems share only the wall line and the
  storey height. **Modelling rule: the mixed frame is a stack of systems, and the storey boundary is
  an authored plane, not a coincidence of grids.**
- **干栏 below, either of the above above.** A 干栏式 undercroft with a 穿斗式 or 抬梁式 house on top is
  the standard south-west section, and the 吊脚楼 is its one-slope variant.

**Side-by-side mixing in one frame.** A widely repeated textbook passage gives the arrangement as *the
gable bays in 穿斗 and the central bays in 抬梁*, so that a wide house gets its hall span without
losing the light frame at the ends [S11b, unverified — it reproduces a construction-textbook passage
and no academic or primary source for it was obtained this session]:

> 「有的需要较大空间的建筑，采取将穿斗式构架与抬梁式构架相结合的办法：**山墙部分使用穿斗式构架，当中
> 的几间用抬梁式构架**，彼此配合。」

**Modelling rule:** the two systems meet on a *plane*, so the transition is a member that belongs to
one system and bears the other — in practice a 梁 whose head lands on a 穿斗 post. The generator needs
a **per-bay system tag**, not a per-building one.

**A textbook set of 穿斗 dimensions**, quoted from the same [unverified] repost and marked as such
because it is repeated everywhere and was **not** confirmed against a primary or academic source this
session [S11b]:

| quantity | value as printed | tag |
|---|---|---|
| 柱径 (post diameter) | 20–30 cm | [S11b] [unverified] |
| 穿枋 section | 6 × 12 cm to 10 × 20 cm | [S11b] [unverified] |
| 檩距 (purlin spacing) | generally ≤ 100 cm | [S11b] [unverified] |
| sheathing | **「椽上直接铺瓦，不加望板、望砖」** — tiles go straight onto the rafters | [S11b] [unverified] |
| erection | the whole 排架 is assembled flat on the ground and then raised | [S11b] [unverified] |

**Why the last row matters even though it is unverified:** if the sheathing layer is absent, the roof
section is *two* layers (rafter + tile) rather than three, the purlins are exposed to weather, and the
dead load drops. That single fact changes the roof's thickness parameter and its load. It is therefore
listed as a **must-verify** item in §Not established, not as a rule to implement.

**Negative result worth recording:** no survey located this session quantifies **what proportion of
south-west buildings use each system**, nor gives a distribution of purlin spacings by region. The
statement "穿斗式 dominates the south-west" is repeated in every general survey but the underlying
counts were not obtained. See §Not established.

## Roof forms and the roof profile

### The six forms, in plan and at the eave

Each is described by what happens **at the ridge and at the eave line**. A useful definition: a form
is determined by (a) how many roof planes meet at the ridge, and (b) whether the eave line is a
continuous rectangle, is broken at the corners, or runs around a polygon.

| 汉字 | pinyin | English | plan | eave line | ridge |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 悬山 | xuánshān | overhanging gable (two-sided / "hanging mountain") | plain rectangle | **continuous straight on all four sides**; the gable (山面) projects past the end wall and the rafter tails are exposed | a single straight line; **no hips**; the roof is two planes only |
| 硬山 | yìngshān | flush gable | plain rectangle | continuous straight; the rafter tails are **hidden behind the gable wall**, which is carried up to the roof | single line, two planes |
| 歇山 | xiēshān | gable-and-hip | rectangle with four hips | the **long eave side** is continuous; the hip at each end rises from the corner and meets the long eave at a **hipped end that is lower than the ridge** | one main ridge, short ridges at the ends; **three planes at each hip corner** |
| 庑殿 | wǔdiàn | hip roof (four-hip) | rectangle | continuous all round, hips at all four corners from the eave | **one point in plan** — a single ridge degenerating to a point at the apex; four planes |
| 攒尖 | cuānjiān | polygonal pointed roof | **regular or irregular polygon** (square, hexagon, octagon) | a polygon ring, all sides equal-ish | **a point**, with hip (戗) rafters converging |
| 卷棚 | juǎnpéng | rolled / barrel ridge roof | rectangle, as 悬山 or 硬山 | as its parent | the ridge is a **shallow arc instead of a straight line** — this is the defining feature and nothing else differs |

Sketch in words for the two that matter most for a south-west house:

- **悬山** — plan: rectangle `W × D`. The ridge line runs parallel to `W` at mid-`D`. The two roof
  planes fall to the `W`-direction eaves. At each end, the rafter tails project past the gable wall by
  the gable overhang; the barge-board (博风板) covers them. Eave line: a rectangle. Two planes.
- **硬山** — the same, with the gable wall raised to the roof and the rafter tails buried in it.
- **歇山** — plan: rectangle `W × D`. Ridge line along `W` but shortened by `a` at each end. From each
  ridge end an **hip (戗)** runs down and outward to the plan corner. The remaining rectangle is covered
  by four trapezoid/triangle regions of the top plane. Eave line: rectangle. Planes: main slope (front
  and back, i.e. the two long sides) plus two hip ends.
- **庑殿** — as 歇山 but the ridge shrinks to a **point**: four hips, four planes, no main ridge.

### The profile: this is the part that must be exact

**The profile is not a pitch. It is the sequence of purlin elevations.** Every traditional rule in
this family is a rule for *where the purlins sit*, and the roof surface is what you get when you lay
boards over them. That is why two roofs of the same average pitch can look completely different, and
why a generator must store a **list of purlin stations with heights**, not a single angle.

Three named algorithms, three periods and regions:

| name | pinyin | period / region | what it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 举折 | jǔzhé | Song (1103, 《营造法式》) | one 举 (total rise) then a **halving** fold series |
| 举架 | jǔjià | Qing (《工程做法则例》, 1734) | a **prescribed coefficient per step**, increasing toward the ridge |
| 提栈 | tízhàn | Jiangnan (《营造法原》, 姚廷枫 rewrite of the Suzhou craftsmen's practice, 1918) | a **single coefficient per step**, larger in the south, with named variants |

#### 举折 (Song) — the primary text, verbatim

《营造法式》卷五 · 大木作制度二 · 举折 [S1]:

> 举屋之法：如殿阁楼台，先量前后撩檐方心相去远近，分为三分。从撩檐方背至脊槫背举起一分。如甋瓦厅堂，即四分中举起一分。又通以四分所得丈尺，每一尺加八分。若甋瓦廊屋及瓪瓦厅堂，每一尺加五分；或瓪瓦廊屋之类，每一尺加三分。

And 梁思成's emendation of the same passage adds the two clauses the 识典 text lacks [S1b]:
「若两椽屋，不加；其副阶或缠腰，并二分中举一分」 and 「若亭榭只用瓪瓦者，即十分中举四分」.

Read carefully, this gives **four 举屋 coefficients**, each a *rise over the horizontal distance between
the backs of the front and back 撩檐方*, measured to the back of the 脊槫:

| building type | coefficient, verbatim | value | angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 殿阁楼台 | 分为三分…举起一分 | **0.3333** | 18.43° [derived] |
| 甋瓦厅堂 (barrel-tiled hall) | 四分中举起一分，每一尺加八分 | 0.25 × 1.08 = **0.270** | 15.13° [derived] |
| 甋瓦廊屋及瓪瓦厅堂 | (四分) 每一尺加五分 | 0.25 × 1.05 = **0.2625** | 14.72° [derived] |
| 瓪瓦廊屋 | (四分) 每一尺加三分 | 0.25 × 1.03 = **0.2575** | 14.44° [derived] |
| 甋瓦厅堂 that is 两椽屋 | 不加 | **0.250** | 14.04° [derived] |
| 副阶 / 缠腰 (side aisle, gallery) | 二分中举一分 | **0.500** | 26.57° [derived] |
| 亭榭只用瓪瓦 | 十分中举四分 | **0.400** | 21.80° [derived] |
| 八角/四角斗尖亭榭, 檐檩→角梁底 | 五分中举一分 | **0.200** | 11.31° [derived] |

**[derived] caveat.** The "四分" base for the 甋瓦廊屋/瓪瓦厅堂 and 瓪瓦廊屋 rows is read across from
the 甋瓦厅堂 sentence, which is the standard reading, but the primary sentence does not repeat the
"四分" — it says 「又通以四分所得丈尺」 once and then only gives the 加分. Flagged.

And the fold series, verbatim [S1]:

> 折屋之法：以举高尺丈，每尺折一寸。每架自上递减半为法。如举高二丈，即先从脊槫背上取平，下屋撩檐方背，其上第一缝折二尺。又从上第一缝槫背取平，下至撩檐方背，于第二缝折一尺。若椽数多，即逐缝取平，皆下至撩檐方背。每缝并减上缝之半。如取平，皆从槫心抨绳令紧为则。如架道不匀，即约度远近，随宜加减。

梁思成's emendation spells the series out [S1b]: 「如第一缝二尺，第二缝一尺，第三缝五寸，第四缝二寸五分之类。」

So, with **H = 举高** (total rise from the back of the 撩檐方 to the back of the 脊槫):

> **fold *i* (counted from the ridge) = H / 10 × 2^−(i−1)**, i.e. **0.10 H, 0.05 H, 0.025 H, 0.0125 H, …** [derived from S1, sequence confirmed verbatim by S1b]

**[derived] and stated plainly:** this geometric series sums to at most 0.2·H. It therefore **cannot
account for the whole rise**, and the remaining rise must fall in the step(s) that the series does not
reach — which, read naively, makes the *lowest* step by far the steepest. That is the opposite of the
Song profile that survives in the buildings. **The rule that closes the frame is therefore not
established from this text**, and the received commentaries (陈明达《营造法式大木作制度研究》, 梁思成
《营造法式注释》) are the source that would settle it — neither was obtainable online this session
(see §Not established). **A generator must not implement this series as a complete profile rule until
that is settled.** What *is* safe to implement: the 举屋 coefficients (table above), which are
self-contained, and the *shape* of the fold series — monotonically decreasing toward the eave, i.e. a
**concave (dipping) roof surface**, which every Song, Qing and Jiangnan source agrees on.

#### 举架 (Qing) — prescribed coefficient per step

The Qing rule does not derive the profile from a total rise. **Each purlin step is given its own
coefficient, and the coefficient increases toward the ridge.** The measured meaning of 举 X is:

> **举高 (vertical) = 步架 (horizontal step) × X / 10**

This is confirmed arithmetically by the 《工程做法则例》 worked example, and both published readings of
it are recorded because **they disagree** [S2]:

> 「凡金瓜柱以步架加舉定高低，如步架深肆尺，按陸舉加之，得高貳尺肆寸。」 — step 四尺, 六举 → 高 二尺四寸. 4 × 0.6 = 2.4 ✓
>
> 「凡脊瓜柱，以步架加舉定高低，如步架深肆尺，按捌舉加之，得高叄尺貳寸，又加平水高捌寸壹分…」 — step 四尺, 八举 → 高 三尺二寸, plus 平水 八寸一分. 4 × 0.8 = 3.2 ✓

A second, different transcription circulates for the 脊瓜柱 rule — 「如步架深參尺伍寸，按九舉加之，
得高三尺六寸」 and its variant 「如步架四尺，按九举加之，得高三尺六寸」 [S3]. The second is
arithmetically consistent (4 × 0.9 = 3.6); the first is **not** (3.5 × 0.9 = 3.15). **Recorded as a
textual variant, not as a second rule.** The full 官式 five-step ladder (五举 / 六五举 / 七五举 / 九举 /
十一举) is reported in the general literature [S4] but **no primary or academic text for the complete
ladder was obtained this session** — see §Not established. Do not treat it as a measured rule.

#### 提栈 (Jiangnan) — a single coefficient, larger in the south

提栈 is the Jiangnan carpenter's rule, recorded in 《营造法原》. **The text was not obtained this
session** — see §Not established. What can be stated without fabricating: 提栈 is expressed as a
**coefficient on the 步架 like 举架**, it is **steeper than the Qing coefficients for the same latitude**,
and it is what the Jiangnan and south-western vernacular roofs are actually built to. Its measured
range is **not established here**. A thesis on Jiangnan timber-frame practice exists and its title
records exactly this subject [S5b], but it was not read.

#### South-western variants, and what "vernacular" means for the roof

| term | pinyin | gloss | what it does geometrically |
|---|---|---|---|
| 深出檐 | shēn chūyán | deep eave overhang | 出檐 larger than the official 0.3 × column height [S2]; the rain screen of a monsoon climate, and the shadow that reads as "vernacular" at a glance |
| 披檐 | pīyán | lean-to eave roof | a **secondary roof plane** on one side of the main volume — a skirt, not a second storey |
| 腰檐 | yōoyán | waist eave | a **second, lower eave line** between storeys — the single most characteristic roof of the south-west, and the reason a plate vocabulary fails |
| 重檐 | chóngyán | double eaves | two eave lines on one body, the upper one marking an inter-storey or an intermediate roof |
| 短脊 | duǎnjǐ | short ridge | a **short ridge relative to the plan** — more ridge than a rectangle needs, which is a **non-rectangular plan made from several rectangular roofs** rather than one roof with an odd shape |

**Every one of 腰檐, 重檐 and 披檐 is a second eave line, and the current vocabulary has exactly one
roof.** A generator that cannot hold two roofs with different eave heights on one body cannot draw a
south-west house.

#### The three algorithms side by side

| | **举折** jǔzhé | **举架** jǔjià | **提栈** tízhàn |
|---|---|---|---|
| period / region | Song 1103, official and hall alike | Qing 1734, official | Jiangnan, 民间; the rule the south-west practice descends from |
| input | one total rise **H**, as a fraction of the eave-to-eave span | **one coefficient per step**, ridge-ward | **one coefficient per step** |
| how the curve is made | halving fold series `0.1H·2^−(i−1)` applied from the ridge; **the closing rule is not established from the primary text** | each step's rise is fixed independently; the profile is the polyline through the purlins | same shape of rule as 举架, different coefficients |
| concavity | yes, **凹曲** | yes, **凹曲** | yes, **凹曲** |
| steepness | shallowest of the three (14–18°) | steepest at the ridge (27°–48° at the coefficients attested) | between them; **range not established** |
| primary source read | **[S1]**, [S1b] — read in full | **[S2]**, [S3] — worked example read; ladder not | **not obtained** |
| can it be implemented today? | **the 举屋 table yes; the fold series no** | **yes**, with the three attested coefficients 五/六/八举 | **no** |

**The generator's honest position:** it can implement 举架 today (three coefficients are attested and
verified arithmetically), it can implement the 举屋 table today, and it **cannot** implement 举折 or 提栈
until the closure and the coefficients are sourced. A `profile_rule` enum with three variants plus a
`measured` variant — where a modeller can simply type in a list of purlin heights — is the honest
shape, and `measured` is the variant that will actually be used for the south-west until the two
missing rules are settled.

### The eave, and the rafters, from the two official texts (contrast)

《营造法式》卷五 · 椽檐 [S5]:

- 「造檐之制，皆从撩檐方心出。如椽径三寸，即檐出三尺五寸；椽径五寸，即檐出四尺至四尺五寸。」
  — **the overhang is measured from the centre of the 撩檐方**, not from the wall face. Fitting the two
  published examples: **出檐(寸) = 5 × 椽径(寸) + 20** [derived] (3寸 → 35寸 ✓; 5寸 → 45寸 ✓, the
  four-foot option being the lower bound).
- 「檐外别加飞檐，每檐一尺，出飞子六寸。」 — **the flying eave adds 0.6 × the overhang** [derived], so
  total eave projection = 1.6 × 出檐.
- 「凡飞子，如椽径十分，则广八分，厚七分。」 — flying rafter section = 0.8 d × 0.7 d.
- 「其稀密，以两椽心相去之广为法。殿阁广九寸五分至九寸，副阶广九寸至八寸五分，厅堂广八寸五分至
  八寸，廊库屋广八寸至七寸五分。」 — **椽档, centre to centre**, by building class.
- 「椽每架平不过六尺；若殿阁，或加五寸至一尺五寸。」 — **maximum step (架) 6尺**.
- 「径九分至十分…若厅堂椽，径七分至八分；余屋，径六分至七分。」 — rafter diameter by class.
- 「若一间生四寸，三间生五寸，五间生七寸。」 — the eave is **deepened toward the corner** as the
  corner bay count rises (angle treatment).

The Qing numbers for the same things are in §The parts (column/purlin/rafter relationships) and differ
in form: Qing gives everything as a **fraction of a governing dimension** (0.8 × 面阔 for column
height, 0.07 × 面阔 for diameter, 0.3 × purlin diameter for rafters, 0.3 × column height for the
overhang) [S2], whereas Song gives them as **absolute 寸 values per class**. A vernacular generator
should use neither table: it should use measured distributions. Those are §Not established.

### (b) The constructs the flat-plate vocabulary lacks, named

1. **A member.** A beam or post with a section, a start, an end and two named joints. The current
   vocabulary has *volumes* and *faces*; it has nothing that can be a 3 m round log 200 mm across whose
   end grain is not visible in any view. **Every timber member in a vernacular house is a member, not a
   wall and not a plate.**
2. **A frame graph.** Posts + ties + beams + purlins + rafters as a graph with **named joints**
   (榫卯 / 节点), so that a joint can be *authored* (this beam's head lands on that post) rather than
   found by an intersection test. This is the same discipline the vegetation survey arrives at for
   branch junctions, and the reason is identical: a search is not authorable.
3. **A station-ordered profile.** A roof is a surface lofted over an **ordered list of purlin stations**,
   the way `mountain`'s `Station` and `Sampling` already work. Rings *i* and *i+1* of the roof surface
   correspond by construction; the profile curve is the list. **This is the missing primitive**: a
   pitched, curved, corbelled roof cannot be produced by offsetting a plate in plan.
4. **A ridge line as an entity**, which may be a segment, a point, or an arc. 攒尖 has no ridge *segment*
   at all, only an apex point, and 庑殿 is the limiting case — so the roof's top cannot be modelled as a
   plate's top edge either.
5. **An eave line as an entity**, separate from the wall line, at its own height, projecting beyond it
   by `eave_overhang`, and carrying rafter tails. The eave is the **only** place the roof touches the
   outside world and it is where 出檐, 椽档, 檐口高度 and the tile's first course all live.
6. **A gable closure.** 悬山 has an exposed rafter tail field and a 博风板; 硬山 has a wall raised into
   the roof. These are different solids occupying the same plan region — a boolean per form, not a
   parameter.
7. **A hip (戗).** 歇山, 庑殿 and 攒尖 each need a member that runs from the ridge to a plan corner,
   with its own section and its own relationship to the purlins.
8. **A secondary roof.** 腰檐, 重檐 and 披檐 are roofs in their own right with their own eave line. A
   data model with a single `roof` slot cannot express the most characteristic south-west silhouette.
9. **A stilt level.** `floor_level ≠ ground_level`. The generator needs an undercroft as an authored
   exterior, with braces, and a floor whose bearing is a beam rather than the terrain.
10. **A wall as infill, with its own extent.** In a 穿斗式 frame the wall panel is bounded by two post
    faces, and the post is *outside* the wall. The plan outline is therefore the post ring, not the wall.
11. **Non-planar offsetting for the frame.** A column has 侧脚 and 收分; a beam has a camber (卷杀 /
    杀梭) and often a 蜀柱-shaped soffit. Neither is a plan offset.
12. **A rafter field as a countable set.** 椽档 is a spacing, the rafter count must be an integer, and
    the Qing text says explicitly 「每間椽數俱應成雙檔之寬窄，隨數均勻」 — the number of rafters per
    bay must fill an even number of spaces. A generator needs a `count` and a `remainder rule`, not a
    spacing alone.

### Recommendation, in the order the work should be done

1. **Add a member and a frame graph before anything else.** Nothing in §Roof profile is expressible
   without posts, beams and purlins existing as members with named joints. This is the smallest change
   with the largest effect and it is what the 穿斗 bay logic ([S11a]) directly demands.
2. **Add a station-ordered roof surface next**, parameterised by a **list of purlin rows**, with the
   `profile_rule` enum and a `measured` variant that a modeller can type into. Do **not** implement
   举折's fold series: the closing rule is not established (§Not established 1). Implement 举架 with the
   three verified coefficients and 举屋 from the [S1] table, both tagged as **official contrast**.
3. **Make the eave a first-class entity and the roof a list.** 腰檐 / 重檐 / 披檐 need a list of roofs;
   深出檐 needs the eave line to be independent of the wall line.
4. **Separate `floor_level` from `ground_level` and model the undercroft** as an exterior surface with
   its own braces and access. This is the 吊脚楼/半吊式 case that the surveyed Chongqing example is
   built from [S11a], and it is the change that most obviously cannot be retrofitted later.
5. **Take every vernacular default from a measured drawing, not from this note.** §Numbers is
   overwhelmingly official. The one vernacular dataset here is one 1916 complex at one site. **Write
   the survey set into the repository before the generator's vernacular defaults are fixed**, because
   every number that gets fixed first will be an official number wearing a vernacular label.

## Not established this session

1. **The closing rule of 举折.** The fold series `0.1H · 2^−(i−1)` sums to at most 0.2H, so it cannot
   by itself produce the total rise, and the residual is not stated in the primary text. Every statement
   above about the Song profile's *closure* is therefore withheld. **Settling measurement:** the full
   text of 陈明达《营造法式大木作制度研究》(中国建筑工业出版社, 1955/1958), the 举折 section, which gives
   the worked purlin-elevation table; second-best, a measured-drawing set (测绘图) with the 脊檩/金檩
   elevations of a Song hall listed.
2. **The complete Qing 举架 ladder.** 五举 for the eave step, 六举 for the 金 step and 八举 for the 脊
   step are attested in the 《工程做法则例》 worked example [S2], and 五举/六五举/七五举/九举/十一举 is
   reported in the general literature [S4] as a **five-step ladder**. The ladder was not found in a
   primary or academic text here. **Settling measurement:** the 庑殿 (or 歇山) 大木 chapter of
   《工程做法则例》, which states the coefficients for a real five-step frame.
3. **提栈 — its formula and its range.** Neither 《营造法原》 nor any academic treatment was obtained.
   This is the rule the target region most needs and it is currently a **name with no numbers**.
   **Settling measurement:** 《营造法原》(中国营造学社, 1925 reprint of 姚廷Coehnship, 1918) 屋面 /
   提栈 chapter; or the Jiangnan thesis [S5b].
4. **Every vernacular south-west dimension except the two sites below.** Only **桥梁民居, 黔江** [S11a]
   (面阔 / 进深 / courtyard dimensions, one 1916 Tujia 穿斗 + 悬山 complex) and the Guizhou
   municipal purlin figures [S16] were obtained. **No 开间, 柱径, 柱高, 檩径, 椽径, 椽档, 出檐,
   檐口 height, 台基 height, wall thickness or door/window proportion exists in this note from a
   surveyed vernacular house.** Everything else in §Numbers is **official** (Song or Qing) and is
   marked as such; **none of it is a vernacular value and none may be used as one.** **Settling
   measurement:** a measured drawing set (测绘图) for at least 30 houses across 洪江 (Hunan),
   西江千户苗寨 (Guizhou), 朗德上寨, 镇远古城, 龙脊梯田 and 丽江白沙; plus 张俭 2006 [S12] on
   vernacular roof pitch vs rainfall, and 叶阳 等 2025 [S17] on the five Yunnan timber house types.
5. **Whether the 穿斗 roof has a 望板 layer in the south-west.** The textbook passage says tiles go
   directly onto the rafters [S11b, unverified]; 营造法式 [S5] and 工程做法则例 [S2] both specify 望板.
   This changes the roof's thickness, its dead load and its purlin exposure, so it **must** be settled
   before a roof thickness parameter is chosen. **Settling measurement:** a section through a surveyed
   穿斗 roof in Chongqing/Guizhou showing the layers between 檩 and 瓦.
6. **The quantitative distribution of structural systems by region.** "穿斗式 dominates the south-west"
   is asserted in the survey literature but **no building counts were obtained**. The Qianjiang survey
   actually says both 抬梁 and 穿斗 occur there [S11a], which is a caution against the claim.
   **Settling measurement:** a typological inventory with counts, such as a 传统民居 national survey
   dataset.
7. **营造尺 and 鲁班尺 in metres.** Both are quoted from memory in the literature at values that
   disagree between sources, and the one document that gives both 尺 and mm [S16] is **internally
   inconsistent** (314 mm vs 309 mm implied). **No number was written into this note**, deliberately.
   **Settling measurement:** an authoritative published conversion with the authority stated, or a
   physical 营造尺 against which the Song and Qing values can be checked independently.
8. **Tile: 小青瓦 / 石板瓦 dimensions, 瓦垄 spacing, overlap, weight per m², and minimum pitch per
   covering.** Entirely absent. This matters more than it looks: **石板瓦 on battens is the common
   covering in Guizhou/Guangxi/Yunnan**, and it changes both the dead load and the minimum pitch. The
   Qianjiang houses are 青瓦 [S11a], which is the *other* covering. **Settling measurement:** a regional
   做法 survey or a tile-yard specification; plus a weigh-bridge figure for dead load.
9. **台基 / 阶条石 / 踏步 / 柱础 / 墙厚 / 格扇 支摘窗.** The 石作 and 装修 chapters of
   《工程做法则例》 and the 小木作 chapters of 《营造法式》 were not read. Only the 楼梯 figures from
   [S2] are quoted, and those are a *stair*, not a *platform*.
10. **The 杖杆 set-out as a rule.** Its existence is documented [S8][S10]; its arithmetic is not.
    **Settling measurement:** a survey of rulers used on site against the modules actually cut, in at
    least one 吊脚楼 building account.
11. **Gable overhang (悬山 出际) in the vernacular.** The official texts give the *eave* overhang only.
    The gable projection has no number here, and 悬山 is the confirmed roof form at the one surveyed
    Chongqing site [S11a], so this gap sits directly on the target type.
12. **翼角起翘 (eave-corner upturn).** 《营造法式》中翼角檐细部处理及起翘探讨 was located and the host
    failed (502). The 起翘 is one of the strongest visual signatures of the south-west eave and this
    note therefore says nothing about it. **Settling measurement:** that paper, plus a measured drawing
    of a 湘黔 vernacular corner.
13. **The regional boundary between 悬山 and 硬山 in the south-west**, which determines the gable
    closure and is a one-bit difference in the generator. Not established. The Qianjiang survey
    names 悬山 and 歇山 for that district and does not mention 硬山 [S11a], which is weak evidence for
    悬山 but from a single site.
14. **Whether a south-west 歇山 is in scope.** The Qianjiang survey states that district's roofs are
    「或歇山式顶，或悬山式顶」 [S11a], and a Guizhou heritage document states 歇山 is used for 木构吊脚楼.
    **The generator must express 歇山 even if the modal form is 悬山**, because the hip is what makes a
    south-west silhouette read as one. No measured 歇山 吊脚楼 was obtained.
15. **腰檐 (waist eave) dimensions.** Named as a defining south-west feature and asserted to exist; **no
    source for its geometry was obtained this session**, so §Roof profile carries it as vocabulary
    only. **Settling measurement:** a section through a 2–3-storey Chongqing 吊脚楼 showing two eave
    lines on one body — the 桥梁民居 survey [S11a] describes 「二楼三面有廊道」, which implies a
    secondary roof but does not dimension it.
